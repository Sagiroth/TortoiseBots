# Available Bot Commands & Addon Controls

TortoiseBots provides a native, intent-driven command suite (`.bot`) that powers both the **[TortoiseBotsManager](https://github.com/Sagiroth/TortoiseBotsManager)** (`/tbm`) addon and standard in-game chat commands.

Bot-targeted commands require the requesting player to own the target bot (same account) or be a GameMaster. `.bot role self` changes only the requesting player's group-role override and does not target a bot.

### Who may use what (issue #294)

Whisper commands (`/w <BotName> <command>`) and `.bot command <BotName> <command>` pass one gate in `PlayerbotAI::HandleCommand`, keyed by the command's first word. GM means the sender's session security is above `SEC_PLAYER`; owner means the sender's account matches the bot's durable owner account (`BotManager` ownership record). Random bots grouped with a player treat that player as a group member, not an owner. Unknown commands default to owner-or-GM (fail closed).

| Tier | Who | Commands |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| GM only | GameMasters (owner NOT included) | `cheat`, `debug` (incl. remote `debug <…>` diagnostics), `cdebug`, `cs`, `log`, `set value`, `teleport`, `load ai`, `save ai`, `list ai`, `reset ai` / `reset strats` / `reset values` |
| Owner or GM | Same-account owner, or any GM | `sendmail`/`mail`, `bank`/`gb`, `ah`/`ah bid`/`ah cancel`, `t`/`trade`/`nt`, `s`/`sell`, `b`/`buy`/`bb`, `repair`, `destroy`, `drop`, `e`/`equip`, `ue`/`unequip`, `keep`, `u`/`use`, `craft`, `guild` + guild shorthand (`gi`/`gk`/`gl`/`gp`/…), `invite`/`join`/`lfg`/`leave`, `summon`, `taxi`, `co`/`nc`/`de`/`react`/`all` strategy changes, `talents`, `reset`, `release`/`revive`/`corpse run`, `trainer`, `skill`, `faction`, `outfit`, `go`, `range`, `flag`, `speak`, `cast`/`castnc`/`spell`, `pet`, `buff`, `share`, `accept`, `talk`, `q`/`quests`, `rep`, `roll`, `ll`, `ss`, `chat`, `home`, `logout`, `do`/`d` item-use shortcuts, `doquest`, `grind`, `r`/`reward`, `bg free`, `move style`, `give leader`, `focus`/`boost`/`follow`/`revive` target assignments, `ra` |
| Group member (tactical/info) | Anyone currently grouped with the bot | `follow`, `stay`, `guard`, `free`, `wander`, `flee`, `runaway`, `attack`, `pull`, `tank attack`, `rti`, `formation`, `stance`, `save mana`, `max dps`, `possible attack targets`, `attackers`, `position`, `who`, `where`, `wts`, `stats`, `c`/`items`/`inv` count, `spells` list, `hire` (self-gated to random bots), `emote`, `help`, `warning`, `ready`, `rebuff`, `queue`, `los`, `wait`, `jump` |

The remote commander (section 1a, off by default) adds one rule: a commander bound to a bot-only party with `.bot commander bind` counts as a member of that party for the tactical `.bot cmd` orders.

Refused commands answer with a short reason in chat; addon-originated requests additionally receive exactly one `TBM:ACTION_ERR|<command>|denied|<reason>` line so the `/tbm` UI can surface it.

**Pool-bot trading (issue #469):** a masterless pool bot refuses a real-player stranger's trade window (`AiPlayerbot.PoolBotTradeMode = 0`, the default) and ignores Trade-channel chatter unless the speaker addresses it directly. Owned and hired bots always trade with their master; `1` limits the pool to master/group/hire-master trades, `2`/`3` allow buy-only/sell-only, `4` restores unrestricted trading. Bot-to-bot trade life (RPG giveaways, enchants, `WTS`/`WTB`) is untouched.

## 1. Tactical Party Actions (`.bot action <intent>`)

Hazard-avoiding chase movement keeps an idle movement generator while its detour spline runs, so the next movement update has a valid default.

Inside a dungeon or raid, a ranged reach (`reach spell`, `reach party member to heal` / `to resurrect` / `for totem`) whose target is in sight walks the navmesh path towards the target and stops at the first point within casting distance that still sees it. The core's chase refuses a non-melee destination inside instances, so those bots used to stand still out of range. Melee reach and the open world keep the chase.

Inside a dungeon or raid, a bot stuck on a cut-off platform (a raised dais the navmesh does not link to the floor) steps down off the edge: when its reach or follow path is partial and ends at its feet, and no mob is hitting it in melee, it looks for floor 2-8 yd lower and about 8 yd towards its target that is walkable navmesh (not lava or slime, no hazard), is reachable over a clear edge, and has a complete path to the target. It then moves flat out over the edge and down, without pathfinding. Each reach and follow action paces its own looks: after 2 s standing still, at most every 3 s; three looks that find no floor, or three steps for the same target, stop it for 30 s. No setting; never outside instances.

Inside a dungeon or raid, out of combat, `follow` no longer trusts a server follow movement that stands still: a bot more than 3 yd beyond its follow distance that has not moved a yard for 2 s (and is not eating, drinking, casting or rooted) tries the step-down above and otherwise walks the navmesh path to its leader itself, at most every 2 s (up to 8 s while no walk can start), until it is back within range. It leaves a bot that eats, drinks, casts or is rooted alone, and gives up for 60 s after five walks in a row that cannot start. With `AiPlayerbot.DungeonFollowHoldBeforePacks` off (the default) the walk can pass an unpulled pack, as far follow already can. Out of combat a healer's `reach party member to heal` only fires for a member below `AiPlayerbot.AlmostFullHealth`, so a full-health tank no longer pulls the healer away from `follow`.

The modern control plane operates on **player intent**. Instead of micromanaging each bot, you issue high-level tactical commands; the server resolves target scope and selects appropriate bot executors:

| Command | Target Required | What It Does |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| `.bot action attack` | Hostile Target | Controllable party DPS/tank bots engage your current target immediately (dedicated healers keep healing). A creature in evade mode — one the core has sent back to its spawn point, absorbing every point of damage aimed at it — is refused with "is evading": nothing can hurt it until it stops moving. The order also lifts an earlier "unreachable" give-up on that mob and its kind (a bot that gave up on one Silvermane Wolf otherwise ignored every wolf that was not hitting it for five minutes); a mob that really cannot be reached is given up on again after 15 s. A dead target (a corpse left selected after the kill) is refused with `dead-target`; the same applies to `interrupt`, `pull` and `pullback`. |
| `.bot action interrupt` | Casting Hostile | Evaluates party bots and orders the first party bot with a ready interrupt (e.g. *Kick*, *Pummel*, *Earth Shock*, *Counterspell*); if it is out of range the bot first closes distance and then casts. |
| `.bot action stop` | None | Clears combat queues and stops current attacks. |
| `.bot action flee` | None | Break off: bots drop combat (and any pull hold) and follow you in passive mode without attacking. The next tactical order — `attack`, `pull`, `pullback`, `focus skull`, `follow`, `stay` — ends the passive hold; a pull brings the whole party back. |
| `.bot action pull [seconds]` | Hostile Target | Directs the party tank to walk up to your target on its own and pull it in melee; the tank keeps the pulled mob as its target and runs its normal rotation (building threat) without a second order. Other bots hold at your position for `seconds` (0-60, default 10) after the tank has engaged, then join. Healers keep healing throughout. Released early by `.bot action attack` / `stop`. The wait ends when its time is up even if the fight is already over, a bot died or you gave it a movement order (`follow`, `stay`, …) in the meantime, so it never outlasts its own time (before, such a wait could stay on and hold the bot back at the start of every later fight); it is not saved with the bot, and a `co +wait for attack` or `wait for attack time` you set yourself is never removed or changed by it (the pull's delay only applies while it runs). A second `pull` while the first mob is still dying keeps its own wait from its own landing. The ACK reports the melee approach as `body-pull`. A pull that draws no aggro (evade, 15 s without an arrival) ends as a failed pull: the tank drops the target, leaves combat and follows you again, and the waiting bots are released at once. A mob that comes to the tank and hits it in melee before the pull action ran (a corner pull, where the tank walks to a mob it cannot see) ends the pull at once and the tank fights it, instead of standing muted until the 15 s cap. The tank's own automatic dungeon pulls still open from range when it has a working ranged option. A pull the tank cannot make is refused with its reason before any bot is touched (nobody is parked, a flee is not ended): `dead-target`, `friendly` (friendly to the tank — for example another player's pet), `too-far` (more than three times `ReactDistance` from the tank, 450 yd by default) or `invalid-target` (not attackable, evading, out of the tank's sight, or a player); `failed` remains only for a refusal those checks do not cover. With no free tank the pull is refused as `no-tank` (no tank-capable owned party bot) or, when a tank-capable bot is alive but **in combat** (a tank cannot start a pull while it fights), as `tank-busy`: wait for the fight to end and send the pull again, or send `.bot cmd attack` at the mob instead. |
| `.bot action pullback [seconds]` | Hostile Target | Tank approaches only as close as needed, pulls (from range when it has a working ranged option — standing still until the shot lands; a ranged weapon the tank cannot actually shoot with, i.e. its shoot spell is missing from the spellbook, does not count — otherwise walking in and hitting the mob in melee once, reported as `body-pull` in the ACK), then returns to your position, holds there and picks the mob up when it arrives. All other bots stand at that spot and attack `seconds` (0-60, default 5) after the tank is back (that wait ends on time the same way as for `pull`). The tank holds the anchor only for that fight — once it is over (mob dead, no live target, out of combat) it resumes following you. Released early by `.bot action attack` / `stop`. A pullback that draws no aggro parks nobody: no anchor hold is placed, the tank follows you again and the waiting bots are released at once. A ranged pull walks to a firing position with a point move (the core's chase cannot carry a puller to shooting range inside a dungeon) when that point is reachable, on the tank's own floor and in sight of the mob; otherwise the tank advances towards the mob until it can shoot, and one already in range with line of sight does not reposition. If the move cannot start at all, the command reports `unreachable` instead of acknowledging. A target the tank cannot pull is refused up front with the same reasons as `pull` (`dead-target`, `friendly`, `too-far`, `invalid-target`), and the legacy `.bot pullback` names the reason in chat. |
| `.bot action come` | None | All bots sprint directly to the player's exact coordinates. |
| `.bot action stay` | None | Bots halt at their current position and hold ground. A later order that sends them at a target (`attack`, `pull`, `pullback`, `focus skull`, `interrupt`, `cc` with a target, the per-bot `.bot attack <Name>`, or a whispered `attack`) ends the stay of every bot it sends: they fight, then follow you again. `hold`/`comestay` are aliases of *come* and move every bot to your current coordinates. After *come*, *hold* or *comestay* the bots are left in `move=stay` at your spot (shown in `TBM:BOTSTATE`), which replaces any earlier stay spot; send `.bot action follow` to bring them back. |
| `.bot action follow` | None | Bots break current movement and resume tight follow formation behind the leader. With `AiPlayerbot.DungeonFollowHoldBeforePacks = 1`, a bot more than 30 yd behind in a dungeon (nobody in combat) instead stops just outside the aggro range of the first unpulled pack on its way and whispers you (its master) `Pack in the way: <name>`; it follows again once the pack is dead or you come back within 25 yd. |
| `.bot action focus skull` | Enemy / None | Sets or targets the **Skull** raid icon; orders all party DPS bots to focus fire on that target. |
| `.bot action cc <mark> [bot]` | Owned Bot / Marked Mob | Assigns the mark to one bot (**exclusive ownership**: any other owned party bot holding the mark is reset to `none`). An explicit bot name assigns that bot without juggling targets; otherwise a targeted owned bot is assigned, else a capable bot is selected server-side (Mage *Polymorph*, Rogue *Sap*, Warlock *Banish*/*Fear*, Priest *Shackle Undead*, Druid *Hibernate*/*Entangling Roots*, Hunter *Freezing Trap*/*Scare Beast*, Paladin *Turn Undead*). Unknown names fail with `no-bot`. Inside non-raid dungeons the bot CCs only its assigned mark; in the open world it may CC a free pick, but never a mob a group member or pet is attacking or targeting (only an explicit mark may CC that one). Never CCs over an existing CC, and stops CCing once only CC-marked mobs are left fighting the group (sheeped or free): the tank and DPS then kill them last instead of standing idle. A stunned, rooted or feared unmarked mob still counts as an enemy left. The assigned mark always wins the bot's CC pick (in the open world a free pick never replaces it). The party leaves the mark alone while it is crowd-controlled, under the CC bot's cast bar, or in its first 10 s of combat with the CC bot ready and in range (a pack's fight starts at the pull): no target pick, area spell (instant or lingering), enemy-healer interrupt or running *Blade Flurry* touches it then (the rogue also keeps *Blade Flurry* off near a mark whose CC bot is ready, after the 10 s too); a free mark (nobody can CC it, or still free after those 10 s) is fought like any other enemy. The CC bot never hits its own mark with an area spell while other enemies are left. A held mark that is not sheeped yet and hits a healer is *Taunted* by the tank (Taunt only, the tank keeps its target). The CC cast starts only in line of sight and 5 yd inside the spell's range (the bot walks closer first). A bot on `stay` cannot walk closer, so it starts no CC from 25-30 yd. With `AiPlayerbot.EnableActionLog = 1`, failed CC casts are written to the bot action log (`CC_CAST_FAILED` rows). With an enemy targeted, the mark is placed on that target (moved off any mob that carried it); with a bot or nothing targeted, the existing mark stays where it is. A selected enemy that is dead, out of the world or not attackable is refused with `no-target`. Any refused order (`no-bot`, `no-target`, `no-cc`, `no-ai`, `failed`) leaves the group's marks and the saved CC owners as they were. A rogue whose master is a real player Saps its mark before the pull: out of combat it stealths, sneaks up and Saps the marked humanoid (on by default, switch with `.bot action sap on\|off`). While its mark is alive and in sight, the CC bot does not sit down to top up its health from `AiPlayerbot.MediumHealth` (70%) or more (it still eats below that), and no bot starts eating or drinking while the group is fighting. |
| `.bot action cc clear [bot]` | Owned Bot / None | Dismisses CC ownership: an explicit name clears only that bot; otherwise a targeted owned bot is cleared, else all owned live party bots. The `TBM:CC_ASSIGN` snapshot drops cleared owners. |
| `.bot action auto cc [on\|off]` | None | With no argument it **toggles** (on becomes off and back). Opt-in smart auto CC (**OFF by default**): a CC-capable bot on its own sheeps a loose add that is hitting a party healer/caster (never the tank), that nobody in the group is attacking or targeting (members + pets), that carries no periodic-damage aura, and that is not the last enemy, skull-marked, or the tank's target. One bot per mob and one auto target per bot; a broken sheep is never re-sheeped once DoT'd/attacked. Explicit `.bot action cc` marks always win; the toggle bypasses the 5-man dungeon mark gate while ON, and while ON this loose add is the only target the bot picks on its own (no legacy free CC anywhere). The stage-1 AoE interlock protects the auto-sheeped mob. Persisted per bot like the loot toggle. |
| `.bot action sap [on\|off] [bot]` | Owned Bot / None | Pre-pull *Sap* on the rogue's CC mark (**ON by default**; see `.bot action cc` above): `off` stops the rogue from stealthing up to its mark and Sapping it before the pull, `on` turns it back on, bare `sap` toggles. An explicit bot name sets only that bot; otherwise a targeted owned bot, else all owned party rogues (other classes are skipped; no rogue in scope fails with `no-rogue`). The ACK detail is the resulting state (`on`, `off` or `mixed`). `off` adds the rogue's non-combat opt-out strategy `no sap` (shows in `nc ?`) and `on` removes it; persisted per bot like the loot toggle. |
| `.bot action aoe [on\|off]` | None | With no argument it **toggles** (the `ACK` detail is the resulting state). AoE is **on by default**. `aoe off` stops every area spell for the scoped bots until `aoe on` (bare `aoe` flips it), persisted per bot like the loot toggle and shared with `.bot behavior <bot> aoe`. Off blocks: mage Arcane Explosion, Blast Wave, Blizzard, Cone of Cold, Flamestrike, Frost Nova; warlock Rain of Fire, Howl of Terror; priest Holy Nova, Psychic Scream; druid Hurricane, Swipe, Demoralizing Roar, Challenging Roar; hunter Multi-Shot, Volley, Carve, Explosive Trap, Frost Trap; paladin Consecration, Holy Wrath; shaman Chain Lightning, Magma Totem, Fire Nova, Earthquake; warrior Cleave, Whirlwind, Sweeping Strikes, Thunder Clap, Demoralizing Shout, Challenging Shout, Intimidating Shout, Piercing Howl; rogue Blade Flurry; War Stomp; Goblin Sapper Charge, grenades and Oil of Immolation; and the `dps aoe` cluster target picker. Single-target spells, dots spread on other attackers, heals and Smoke Bomb are unaffected (it is the `no aoe` strategy, a veto on those actions by name). `aoe off` also wins over `co +aoe fear`. `aoe on` only lifts the block; it no longer adds the `dps aoe` cluster target picker, which stays available as `co +dps aoe`. With AoE on, AoE triggers additionally refuse to fire while a deliberately CC'd mob (Polymorph, Sap, Gouge, Shackle Undead, Hibernate, Freezing Trap, Seduction, Repentance, Wyvern Sting) is in or beside the pack, so a stray sheep is not broken even with AoE left on. Frost Nova does not block AoE. |
| `.bot action loot [on\|off]` | None | Toggles corpse looting across scoped bots; with no argument it flips the current state, and the `ACK` detail is the resulting state. |
| `.bot action repair` | None | Orders scoped bots to repair gear at a nearby vendor. |
| `.bot action sell` | None | Orders scoped bots to sell grey vendor trash. |
| `.bot action rest` *(or `drink`, `eat`)* | None | Orders scoped bots to sit and consume food/drink until full. When no bot starts, the refusal groups the bots by reason (`TBM:ACTION_ERR\|rest\|failed\|No bot rested: no drink: <Name>, <Name>; in combat: <Name>.`, cut at about 240 characters with `+K more`): `dead`, `in combat`, `party still fighting`, `no drink` / `no food` / `no food or drink` (no item in the bags and no item cheat; a mage in the party trades conjured water), `leader is moving` (under its stop line but following a moving leader, the bot's own should-drink/should-eat check), `nothing to rest (...)` when it is above the lines it rests to, or `other: not useful now` for any other gate (for example a CC bot holding for its mark). |
| `.bot action release` | None | Commands dead companion bots to release spirit. |
| `.bot action corpse run` | None | Commands spirit bots to run back to corpse. |
| `.bot action learn` | None | Commands scoped bots to learn spells from nearby trainers. |

| `.bot action ready` | None | Initiates a group ready check across all party bots. |
| `.bot action raid status` | None | Reports per-bot raid strategy state (`molten core`, `onyxia`, `four horseman`, `solnius`, … or `outdoor`). |
| `.bot action raid tankface` | Dragon Target | Orders tank bots to drag the current dragon boss away from the raid anchor so breath/cleave miss the raid. |

### 1a. Remote commander (`.bot commander`, `.bot cmd`, issue #884)

Off by default: needs `AiPlayerbot.RemoteCommander = 1`, otherwise both commands answer `TBM:ACTION_ERR|<intent>|disabled|…`. A commander leads a bot-only party of up to five **without being in it**, from anywhere (another zone, outside the instance): the orders name their target by GUID or a 3D point, so they need neither your selection nor your presence in the group. Replies are the usual `TBM:ACTION_ACK|<intent>|<party\|bot:Name>|<count>|<detail>` / `TBM:ACTION_ERR|<intent>|<code>|<message>` lines, with the intent `commander <verb>` or `cmd <verb>`.

**Who may bind:** a GameMaster, or a player who controls every bot of the party (owner account, master or claim: the same check as every `.bot` command). A hired companion **of yours** can be bound (see *Your own hires* below); another player's hire cannot (`hired`: its hire ends when it leaves its hirer's party). A party with a player in it cannot be bound. One commander per party, one party per commander. **Who may order:** the bound commander counts as a member of its party, so the tactical orders below are allowed; `revive`, `summon`, `enter` and the per-bot player orders `loot`, `open` and `use` stay owner-or-GM (they move items, open things or take a bot to another map), and are checked against the bot each time (`enter`: against every bot it moves).

**Your own hires (owner-approved extension of the remote commander, answers the hires question of #898).** `bind` accepts bots that are hires **of the commander** (the commander is their hirer); a hire of anyone else stays refused (`hired`). The usual flow: hire five (see below), then `.bot commander bind <Hire1> <Hire2> <Hire3> <Hire4> <Hire5>`. The commander must not be in the group, so a commanded group is five hired bots and the commander stands outside. Five hires need `AiPlayerbot.HireMaxBotsPerPlayer` of at least 5 and your party converted to a raid (a party holds you and four); `bind` then re-forms the raid as a party of the five hires (the raid and its instance bind are not kept, as for any raid), and a party of you and four hires is kept as described in the `bind` row. Name every hire you want commanded: a hire you leave out stays in your party and, if the party has to disband, ends with it.
* **While bound** the usual hire end "the hire left, or the hirer left, the hirer's party" is suspended for those hires (a narrow exception keyed on the active binding, `HireCommandState` in `runtime/HireDeparturePolicy.h`): the commander is meant to be outside their party. Everything else still ends a hire: `.bot remove`/`.bot logout` on it (dismiss), a removal from the bound party that the bind did not make, and the hirer's logout (below). A disband of the bound party (or the lead bot logging off or leaving it) is not an end: it ends the binding, and the hires are returned to the hirer as on `unbind`. The remote commander's own follow and formation applies in place of the follow-the-hirer behaviour; the hire has no timer or payment that depends on the hirer's party (the fee is paid once at hire, the hourly restock runs on its own clock), so nothing else is suspended.
* **On `unbind`** (or any automatic end of the binding) the hires are **kept and returned to their hirer**: if the hirer is online they leave the bound party and accept the hirer's invitation again (the hirer's party becomes a raid when it needs more than five), and follow the hirer as before. The bound party and its instance bind are not kept: a hire cannot stay in a party its hirer is not in. A hire that is out of world at that moment (a far `cmd summon`, an instance entry) is held and re-joined once it is back, for at most the logout grace period (at least a minute), then the ordinary rules apply. If a hire does not rejoin (for example the invitation was refused) it ends within five seconds as any hire outside its hirer's party does. A bind that fails midway returns the hires the same way.
* **When the hirer logs out** the binding ends (`the commander logged out`) and the hires are on the ordinary logout grace (`AiPlayerbot.HireDisconnectGracePeriod`): they hold where they stand and are deleted when it runs out. If the hirer returns inside the grace they leave the bound party and rejoin the hirer's, as a hire does on a hirer's return. A server restart ends every hire, bound or not.
* **Instance entries and accounts.** A hire is a character on a bot-pool account, not on the commander's account (`HireProvisionService::CreateCandidate` picks a pool account; the commander's account is only recorded as the ledger owner). The core counts a dungeon entry on the account of the entering session (`Player::AddInstanceEnterTime` uses `GetSession()->GetAccountId()`), so the hires' entries and saved instances count against the pool accounts; the commander does not enter and has none.

**Instance bind and the bind step.** A dungeon's instance bind belongs to the **group**, and a bot that leaves its group inside the dungeon is sent to its homebind about a minute later. The core keeps a group's five-man bind when its leader changes (only permanent binds are dropped and re-copied from the new leader) and when a member leaves (the leaver alone starts the homebind timer, and a GameMaster does not), but a group formed afresh has no bind until someone enters the map. That is why `bind` keeps your party instead of rebuilding it (see the `bind` row below); to enter an instance with a commanded group, bind first, then `cmd enter` (below) takes the lead bot in and the others after it; or bring the lead bot in by hand and `cmd summon` the others.

**While bound:** the followers follow the **lead bot**, not their master (their per-bot follow target is pointed at the lead bot and put back when the binding ends; nobody's master changes). Every bot but a tank backs off near the tank's threat, as in a player's party: the binding gives it the `threat` behaviour if it did not have it (also to a bot that joins later) and takes it off again when the binding ends; the binding's `threat` is never stored with the bot. The back-off yields on a mob that is hitting the healer or a damage bot of the party, so the party can take it off them at once. The lead bot holds its spot until you send it somewhere with `.bot cmd move`. Leaving a dungeon yourself does not summon your commanded bots out of it (the summon on leaving a dungeon skips them). The binding ends on `.bot commander unbind`, when you log out, when the party disbands or the lead bot leaves it, loses the lead or logs out, when a player joins the party, when the party becomes a raid or grows past five bots, or when the switch is turned off; an automatic end is reported once as `TBM:ACTION_ACK|commander unbind|party|<n>|<reason>`. A single follower that leaves the party (kicked, invited into its owner's party, or leaving on its own) gets its own follow target back within a second and answers its owner's orders again; the rest of the binding stands. The bots stay grouped after the binding ends and the lead bot keeps holding where it stands. (Your own hires are the exception: they return to your party, see *Your own hires*.) While bound, the bots' own looting leaves game objects (chests, herb and ore nodes, quest objects) alone: they still loot corpses, but open an object only on `.bot cmd loot` or `.bot cmd open` (before, several bots started the 5 s Opening of a chest on their own on arrival, and each cast broke on its next follow step). A bot in a commanded group does **not** leave when its master leaves their party (the general rule that a bot leaves with its master stays for every other bot): leaving your own party, or being taken out of it by the bind, leaves the commanded group intact.

| Command | What It Does |
| :--- | :--- |
| `.bot commander bind <LeadBot> [<Bot> ...]` | Binds the lead bot's party to you. A lead bot already in a bot-only party (not a raid) adopts it as it is (it becomes the party leader); the named bots join it (the lead bot invites them through the normal group handlers). #### Player orders: `loot`, `open`, `use`, `ritual` (and `inv`)

What a player does at a corpse, a chest, a lever or in the bags, done by **one bound bot** on the commander's word, so a remote driver can run a whole dungeon (the chest, the key, the quest item, the combined staff) without being there. They are owner-or-GM orders (`denied` otherwise) and take a bot of the commanded party by name (`no-bot` for any other name). Objects are named by the raw GUID, as `0x` + hex or decimal (the feed's `H` records carry the hostiles' GUIDs and a corpse keeps its creature's GUID; the GUID of a game object is the spawn's, which the driver takes from its own world data).

| Command | What the bot does |
| :--- | :--- |
| `.bot cmd loot <guid\|entry:<id>[@x,y,z]> <bot> [itemId]` | Walks to the corpse or object and loots it. Without `itemId` it takes **everything** the group's loot rules give it (the strategy's "is it worth the bags" filter does not apply to an explicit order; unique items it already holds are skipped, and money is taken); with `itemId` it takes only that item and leaves the money. ACK: `TBM:ACTION_ACK\|cmd loot\|bot:<Name>\|<count>\|<itemId:count,...>` (count = items taken; only items that really reached the bag are listed; the list ends with `copper:<n>` when money came too; `-` when nothing was taken). With an `itemId` that did not come out (not the bot's under round robin or group loot, taken by someone else, no bag room) the answer is `failed`, not an empty ACK. Group loot rules are the core's: under round robin only the member whose turn it is can open a corpse (`failed`: "nothing this bot may loot"); under group loot or need-before-greed an item at or above the threshold goes to a roll and is not handed out here; free-for-all lets anyone take it. Set the method first with `cmd lootmethod`. |
| `.bot cmd open <guid\|entry:<id>[@x,y,z]> <bot>` | Walks to a game object (chest, door, lever, altar, button) and uses it like a player's right click. A locked object the bot cannot open is refused before the walk: `no-item` (it has no key or item row it carries, the ERR says which kind) or `locked` (it lacks the skill, for example lockpicking). Owner or GM for every named bot. All named bots walk to the object like `open` does and hold still where they arrive; once **all** stand in range they use it one after the other (the first bot named uses first, and each use is checked against the object's participants and sent once more if it did not register). The use that brings the count to the ritual's own number (3 for the altars) turns the object active and casts the ritual spell (5 s for the altars: cast time index 6 in SpellCastTimes.dbc), and the bots stay still for 7 s so that cast can run. Bots named beyond that number only walk and hold. From its use on, a bot's own AI does nothing (no spell, buff, heal, drink or step): its helper channel is its participation, and any cast of its own would end it and take it out of the count; every end of the order gives the AI back. Later: `interrupted` (a participant moved more than half a yard, was hit or stunned before the object was active, or the object went back to ready while the cast ran), `in-combat` (a bot is in a fight **before** the object is active: all are let go, nothing was fired), `unavailable`, `unreachable`, `timeout`, `cancelled`, and `failed`. **`failed` with "did not make it a participant"**: the core remembers the **first player ever** to use the object (it is set once and never reset, not even by a failed or finished ritual); that player is the caster and decides which group the later users must belong to. If anyone outside the commanded party clicked the altar before (this instance), every use of the bots is ignored without a word and the order ends `failed` with that text; it is the first user ever, not the first bot named. A fresh instance resets it. Every failure lets all bots go and stops their channel, so the object is not left half used (once the object is active the channel is left alone: a participant leaving then would reset it). The object keeps participants whose channel simply ran out (a channel lasts 10 min), so a repeated order after a long wait can count a bot twice; the core has no timer for a half-used ritual. |
| `.bot cmd state <guid\|entry:<id>[@x,y,z]>` | What a game object is doing now, for a client that cannot see it (a door that opens when a fight is won, the Seal in Uldaman). It answers at once: `TBM:ACTION_ACK\|cmd state\|party\|1\|guid:<decimal>,entry:<id>,type:<gameobject type>,state:<active\|ready\|alternative>,loot:<not-ready\|ready\|activated\|just-deactivated>,flags:<none\|in-use+locked+...>` (no spaces, no `\|`; flags: `in-use` the object is animating and cannot be used, `locked`, `interact-cond`, `no-interact`). `state:active` is a door that is open or a lever that was pulled, `ready` one at rest. **Targets by entry** (`loot`, `open`, the game-object form of `use`, and `state`). A client that never saw an object spawn does not know its GUID; it can name the object by its entry instead: `entry:<id>` or `entry:<id>@<x>,<y>,<z>` (decimal entry 1-16777215, the point written with commas or semicolons and **no spaces**, so `.bot cmd open entry:124371@-235,262,-48 Lcwarlock`). The server picks the **nearest spawned game object** of that entry on the bot's map (for `loot`, also the nearest corpse of a creature of that entry; a corpse with loot left is preferred, one with nothing left is taken only when nothing else is there): with a point, within 30 yd of that point (the point itself must be within `AiPlayerbot.ReactDistance` of the bot, else `too-far` and nothing is searched, so an order cannot ask what is spawned elsewhere on the map; only objects within `AiPlayerbot.ReactDistance` of the bot are ever chosen); without one, within `AiPlayerbot.ReactDistance` of the bot (`state`: of the judge bot). Equally near ones go to the lowest GUID. It is one bounded search of the map cells that radius touches (loaded ones only), not a scan of the world. When nothing is there the answer is `TBM:ACTION_ERR|cmd <verb>|not-found|...`. A chosen object (after the `too-far` and reach checks passed) is announced first by one line `TBM:ACTION_TARGET|cmd <verb>|bot:<Name>|<guid decimal>|<entry>` (before the PENDING and the final ACK, so a client can log which object it got and use that GUID later; a client that does not know the line can ignore it). Entry numbers of game objects and of creatures are separate lists; for `loot` the nearer of the two wins, so give a point when both exist near the corpse. The GUID form works as before; a GUID takes no point.

**Timing: these orders are asynchronous.** The order is checked at once; a refusal is a single `TBM:ACTION_ERR|cmd <verb>|<code>|<message>` line. An accepted order may still finish on the spot (the bot already stands at the object, an instant item): the answer is then the final ACK or ERR alone. Otherwise a `TBM:ACTION_PENDING|cmd <verb>|bot:<Name>|<budget seconds>|<object guid or 0>` line says it is running, and the **final** `TBM:ACTION_ACK` / `TBM:ACTION_ERR` with the same `cmd <verb>` and bot follows later. A client waits for the next ACK/ERR line of that verb and bot, with the budget as its timeout (walk: 10 s plus the distance at 4 yd/s, at most 60 s; then up to 8 s for a loot window (also `open` on a chest), 4 s for an object to react, 15 s for a cast; once the action's cast is under way, its remaining cast time is added). The PENDING budget is reckoned when the order starts, so a client should wait the PENDING budget plus about 6 s for `open` or `loot` on a locked chest (the act adds the Opening cast time). One order runs per bot at a time: a second one answers `busy` until the first has its final line.

**The walk.** The bot holds (`stay`) at a point 1.5 yd short of the object and walks there on the navmesh, then does the one action. The object must be within `AiPlayerbot.ReactDistance` (150 yd) of the bot (`too-far`; move the party in legs with `cmd move`). While it walks the bot follows nobody, so if the carrier is a follower it leaves the formation for the walk and goes back to following the lead bot at the end (a bot that was holding a spot holds where it ends up); if the carrier is the **lead bot**, the party follows it to the object. A later order that covers the bot (`stay`, `follow`, `move`, `attack`, `focus`, `pull`, `pullback`, or `summon` of that bot) cancels its running order with `cancelled`. `stay`, `follow`, `move` and `summon` set the bot's movement themselves; after `attack`, `focus`, `pull` or `pullback` the cancelled bot ends its walk as a finished one does (a follower goes back to following the lead bot, a bot that was holding a spot holds where it stands), so it does not return to the object after the fight. A follower that leaves the commanded party ends its order with `unavailable`. A fight during the walk ends the order (`in-combat`; a `loot` or `open` order is refused at once for a bot already in combat). A `use` with no target or on a unit walks nowhere and is allowed in combat (a potion, a bandage, a grenade); a `use` on a game object walks and stops in a fight like the others. The order also ends with the bot dying or teleporting (`unavailable`), the object vanishing (`invalid-target`), the bot standing still short of the object for 8 s (`unreachable`) or running out of the walk budget (`timeout`). Releasing the binding cancels running orders (`failed`, "released").

**The act.** From the first attempt to start the action until its result is in, the bot holds still where it stands: it stops, its stay spot moves to where it is, a follow or formation step and a spell of its own are dropped, so nothing breaks the cast (the Opening of a locked chest stops at the first step; before this hold the bot's own follow movement interrupted it after about a second, or refused it outright). Only the act's own spell is protected, and a bot that is feared, confused, charmed or stunned is left to the core. A fight ends the act at once with `in-combat` (a `use` with nothing to walk to keeps going), and a refused start is not retried in a fight. The bot's stance comes back with the order's final line (done, `cancelled`, `timeout` or any failure), as after a walk; when the order ends while the bot is between maps, the stance comes back as soon as it stands in the new map. A start the bot refuses is retried for about a second (four looks), then the order answers with the newest reason: `busy` (still moving, casting, or someone else is opening or looting the object), `too-far` (out of the opening range), `invalid-target`, `no-item`, `failed` with the core's spell check number, or `failed` "The bot could not start opening it." when no check was recorded. The order starts the opening itself while the bot holds its stay (before, the bot's loot code skipped any opening while the bot held a stay, so every chest order ended with "spell check 255" before a cast was tried). A started cast that ends before its time without a result (the core cancelled it) answers at once: `failed` "The opening cast was interrupted after <s> s (the bot moved <n> yd / the bot is in combat / it did not move and is not in combat)."

**Codes** (second field of `TBM:ACTION_ERR`): `no-bot` (not a bot of your party), `denied` (not the bot's owner or a GM), `too-far`, `no-item` (key, item or ingredient missing), `locked` (skill missing), `busy`, `not-enough` and `interrupted` (`ritual` only), `in-combat`, `invalid` (malformed arguments), `invalid-target` (no such corpse, object or unit on the bot's map), `not-found` (a by-entry target with no spawned object or corpse in the search area within the bot's reach; `cmd state` also for a GUID that is not a spawned game object within reach), `unavailable` (the bot is dead, on a taxi or teleporting, or left the party; an item that teleports the bot, such as a hearthstone, answers ACK once its cast completed), `cancelled` (a later order took the bot), `unreachable`, `timeout`, `failed` (anything else, the message says what), plus the shared `not-bound`, `disabled`, `lead-unavailable`, `requester`. `cmd enter` adds `requirement` and `instance-limit` (see *Entering a dungeon*).

A GUID is the raw 64-bit object GUID, as `0x` + hex (`0xF130000C5E0012AB`) or a decimal number. It must be a live, hostile, attackable unit on the lead bot's map and instance (`invalid-target`, `dead-target`, `friendly`) that a live bot of the party can see (stealth and invisibility detection as the bots' own targeting does it) and target (`no-target` otherwise: a GUID never reveals a stealthed rogue or an invisible GM); a bot or a party member is never a target. Every order answers `not-bound` without a standing binding and `lead-unavailable` while the lead bot is between maps.

#### Entering a dungeon: `cmd enter`

A headless bot never sends its own `CMSG_AREATRIGGER`, and `.tele` only knows the `game_tele` names (Zul'Farrak has none inside), so a remote driver could not take a party through a dungeon door. `.bot cmd enter <area trigger id|near> [bot]` does what a player does there: walks into the trigger zone and enters.

* **Which door.** `<area trigger id>` is the id of an `areatrigger_teleport` row that leads into a dungeon or raid map from the map the bots stand on. `near` picks the nearest such entrance to the lead bot (to the named bot when one is named), within `AiPlayerbot.ReactDistance`, the lowest id on a tie. The bots must be on the trigger's map and within `ReactDistance` of it (`too-far`: move the group closer with `cmd move`); an id that is not an instance entrance, or no entrance in reach for `near`, is `not-found`. The ACK names no trigger; the PENDING line's last field carries the id that was chosen.
* **How the bot enters.** The bot walks the task walk (a `stay` hold at the middle of the zone, a path step every second when stuck), and once it stands inside the zone it calls `WorldSession::HandleAreaTriggerOpcode` for its own session: the same function a client's packet reaches. Everything the core does for a player there applies, nothing is skipped or copied: the trigger's content phase, level and condition (keys and quests are conditions), the raid-group rule of a raid map, the per-account instance limit (`MapManager::CanPlayerEnter`), the instance and group binds (`Player::TeleportTo`, `DungeonMap::BindPlayerOrGroupOnEnter`), a full or encounter-locked instance (`Map::CanEnter`), and a script's say (`ScriptMgr::OnAreaTrigger`). A hire's entries count against its pool account, as for any entry (see *Instance entries and accounts*). No new core hook is used.
* **Order of entry.** The lead bot goes first: it creates the instance and the group's bind. The followers walk to the door with it but hold there until the lead bot stands in the dungeon, then enter one look after another and so join the same instance. A named bot enters on its own, with no waiting. If the lead bot cannot get in, the others stay out (`cancelled`, folded into the lead's error). Afterwards the bots stand where they arrived and the followers follow the lead bot again.
* **Refusals before anything moves.** The order is checked for every bot first, and nothing moves unless all pass: `no-bot`, `denied`, `busy`, `in-combat`, `unavailable` (dead, on a taxi or teleporting), `too-far`, and the entry's requirements, read ahead so the answer can say why: `requirement` (level, the trigger's condition with its own message when it has one, a raid map without a raid group, the content phase) and `instance-limit` (the account has entered too many instances this hour). The message starts with the bot's name. A GameMaster bypasses the level, condition, raid and limit checks as in the core. Because a commanded party is at most five bots, a raid entrance answers `requirement` ("a raid instance needs a raid group").
* **Async answer.** `TBM:ACTION_PENDING|cmd enter|<party\|bot:Name>|<budget seconds>|<trigger id>` when the order runs on, then **one** final line for the whole order: `TBM:ACTION_ACK|cmd enter|<scope>|<n>|entered` when every bot stands in the dungeon, otherwise `TBM:ACTION_ERR|cmd enter|<code>|<Bot>: <message>` with the first real cause (`requirement`, `instance-limit`, `unreachable`, `timeout`, `in-combat`, `unavailable`, `cancelled`, or `failed` when the core refused for a reason only it sees: the instance is full or locked by an encounter, or a script said no), followed by `(<n> of <m> got in)` when some did. Budget: the walk (10 s plus the distance at 4 yd/s, at most 60 s) plus 30 s for the lead bot's transfer, and another 30 s with followers. Another order that covers a bot (`stay`, `follow`, `move`, `attack`, `focus`, `pull`, `pullback`, `summon`) cancels its entry; a bot that already left stays where it is.
* **Client protocol (remote driver).** Send `.bot cmd enter near` (or `.bot cmd enter <id>`) with the party standing at the door, wait for the one final `cmd enter` line (the PENDING budget plus about 6 s as the timeout), and on ACK treat the party as inside: its `TBM:VIEW` frames then report the dungeon's map and instance id and the usual `cmd move` takes over there. On ERR read the code: `requirement` and `instance-limit` are decisions for the driver (fetch the key, level up, wait for the hour), `too-far` means walk closer first, `timeout` and `unreachable` mean look at the door with `cmd state`/the feed before retrying.

#### Perception feed (`TBM:VIEW`, phase 2)

While you are bound and your view is on, you receive one **frame** about every `AiPlayerbot.RemoteCommanderViewMs` (default 1000 ms): what your bots are, and what they see. It is built only from what the bots already track (their health, power, position, selection and cast; the hostiles they fight or have in sight in their own cached lists; the units attacking them) plus each engaged hostile's own threat list; it never makes the server search the world. Hostiles are deduplicated across the bots and capped at 24 per frame (what they fight first, then what they merely see); a stealthed or invisible unit appears only when a bot of the party detects it. Nothing is sent while no commander is bound or the view is off.

The lines are system messages (the TBM addon hides `TBM:` lines from the chat frame), each at most 250 bytes. A frame goes out at most 8 lines per server tick; if the previous frame is still going out when the next one falls due, that one is skipped, so a slow link never queues frames up.

Grammar, version `v1`. Every line of a frame carries the same `<seq>` (counts up per binding, from 1):

```text
TBM:VIEW|v1|<seq>|B|<lead>,<party>,<hostiles>,<threats>,<truncated>
TBM:VIEW|v1|<seq>|P|<party record>[;<party record>...]
TBM:VIEW|v1|<seq>|H|<hostile record>[;<hostile record>...]
TBM:VIEW|v1|<seq>|T|<threat record>[;<threat record>...]
TBM:VIEW|v1|<seq>|E|<lines>

party record   = guid,name,hp,maxhp,power,maxpower,powertype,x,y,z,o,map,instance,target,spell,dead,state,action,result,reason
hostile record = guid,entry,x,y,z,hp%,target,spell,flags,reaction,kind
threat record  = hostile,guid:pct[,guid:pct...]
```

- `B` (begin) is always the first line: the lead bot, how many party, hostile and threat records the frame holds, and `<truncated>`: `1` when the caps or the line limit left at least one record out of the frame (the counts are what was written), else `0`. `<truncated>` is the fifth field of `B`, added within `v1`: fields 1-4 keep their meaning, and a reader takes a missing fifth field as `0`. `E` (end) is always the last: `<lines>` counts every line of the frame, `B` and `E` included, so a client knows the frame is whole. In between come the `P`, `H` and `T` lines, in that order, each only when it has records; a part may span several lines.
- GUIDs are `0x` + upper-case hex (the form `.bot cmd` takes back), `0` for none. Coordinates have one decimal, the orientation two.
- Party: `powertype` 0 mana, 1 rage, 2 focus, 3 energy; `target` the bot's target (else its victim); `spell` the spell id it is casting or channelling (0 none); `dead` 0/1; `state` `c` combat, `n` non-combat, `d` dead, `r` reaction; `action`, `result` and `reason` are the newest verdict its AI gave an action, as parts. `action` is the action name only (up to 16 bytes, for example `drink`), `-` when none yet. `result` is one letter: `O` ok, `F` failed, `I` impossible, `U` useless, `P` prerequisites queued first, `V` dropped before running (vetoed, or held back by the failure backoff), `-` none. `reason` is a short code for why a failed (`F`) or impossible (`I`) action did not run, when the bot's last spell-cast check said so: `no-item`, `oom`, `range`, `los`, `cd`, `moving`, `casting`, `no-target` (up to 10 bytes); `-` when unknown, which is the case for failures that are not a cast check (an item or movement action that fails reports `F` with no reason). `result` and `reason` are the 19th and 20th fields, added within `v1` as trailing fields: a reader that stops at 18 fields keeps working, and now gets a clean action name where it used to get the head of the AI's rolling log cut to 24 bytes. A party record is about 150 bytes in practice and 204 at the extreme (every number at its maximum), so each fits a line on its own (line prefix at most 26 bytes, limit 250); the action, result and reason together take at most 29 bytes, 5 more than the old 24-byte action. Names are cut at 12 bytes.
- Hostile: `entry` the creature entry (0 for a player); `hp%` 0-100; `target` its target; `flags` any of `s` stealthed, `e` evading, `c` crowd-controlled (stunned, incapacitated, polymorphed, feared, rooted), or `-`.
- `reaction` and `kind` are the 10th and 11th fields of a hostile record, added within `v1` as trailing fields: a reader that stops at 9 fields keeps working (and keeps seeing neutral units in the list). `reaction` is how the bound bot reacts to the unit, `h` hostile, `n` neutral (it does not attack unprovoked: wildlife, quest givers, most town NPCs) or `f` friendly, `-` when unknown; a driver that wants real enemies keeps `h`. `kind` is any of `k` critter (creature type Critter), `v` civilian, `g` guard, `u` not attackable (spawning, immune to players or the unattackable flag), or `-`; a driver that skips ambient life skips `n`, `k`, `v`, `g` and `u`. Both come from fields the unit and its template already hold. Neutral units fill the 24 slots only after every hostile one.
- Threat: for each hostile in combat with a threat list, its top three entries, highest first, as `guid:pct` with `pct` the share of the top entry's threat (the top is 100).
- Free-text fields never contain `|`, `;`, `,` or `:` (they become `_`).

Example (one bot fighting one mob):

```text
TBM:VIEW|v1|12|B|0x2A,1,1,1,0
TBM:VIEW|v1|12|P|0x2A,Tanky,4000,5000,35,100,1,-8913.2,554.5,93.1,1.50,0,0,0xF130000C5E0012AB,0,0,c,heroic strike,O,-
TBM:VIEW|v1|12|H|0xF130000C5E0012AB,3874,-8900.0,560.0,94.0,64,0x2A,0,-,h,-
TBM:VIEW|v1|12|T|0xF130000C5E0012AB,0x2A:100,0x2B:41
TBM:VIEW|v1|12|E|5
```

Flags: `e` the bot can equip it, `u` the item-usage value rates it an upgrade, `b` it cannot be traded to you right now (soulbound/quest), `-` none. |
| `.bot item <Name> trade` | The bot opens a trade window with you. Put items or gold in and accept; the bot accepts on its own. |
| `.bot item <Name> equip <bag> <slot>` | The bot equips that exact bag item (bag/slot as reported by `.bot inv`). Answers an ACK/ERR and a fresh snapshot. |
| `.bot item <Name> unequip <bag> <slot>` | The bot moves that equipped item (bag `255`, slot `0`-`18`) into its bags. Fails with `failed` when the bags are full. |
| `.bot item <Name> give <bag> <slot>` | Puts that bag item into a trade window with you. Without an open trade the bot opens one and answers `pending`; repeat the command once the window shows (the addon does this automatically). Soulbound/quest items are refused up front (`no-trade`) because the core would cancel the whole trade. |
| `.bot behavior <Name> <key> <on\|off>` *(alias `behaviour`)* | Per-bot behaviour toggle, persisted like the loot toggle. Keys: `loot` (loot corpses), `aoe` (all area spells, on by default; `off` adds the `no aoe` strategy, same switch as `.bot action aoe`), `autocc` (`auto cc`), `savemana` (`conserve mana`), `boost` (offensive cooldowns), `threat` (ease off near tank threat), `potions` (reaction engine), `spread` (opt-in combat spacing, off by default), `mimic` (`mimic consumables`, off by default: the bot drinks the class/spec/level-appropriate equivalent when you use an elixir, flask, scroll, weapon imbue, protection potion, stat food, alcohol, raid buff or utility potion out of combat — capped by your item's level band so a cheap lowbie drink never grants BiS). Use `.bot behavior party\|raid mimic on\|off` for the whole group. Small groups (up to 4 bots) whisper what they use; larger raids stay silent and you get one `[Raid Mimic]` summary line. Answers an ACK and the bot's `TBM:BOTSTATE` line. |

Replies use the action protocol: `TBM:ACTION_ACK|item <op>|bot:<Name>|1|<itemId>[ pending]`, `TBM:ACTION_ACK|behavior <key>|bot:<Name>|1|<on|off>` (`.bot behavior party|raid ...` answers `TBM:ACTION_ACK|behavior <key>|party|<N>|<on|off>`), or `TBM:ACTION_ERR|<intent>|<code>|<message>`. The ACK scope is always `party` or `bot:<Name>`, the detail never contains `|`, and the ERR intent is never empty (an unparseable `.bot action` names the first word typed, cut to 32 bytes on a UTF-8 character boundary with `|`, CR and LF turned into spaces, or `unknown` when nothing is left).

## 7. Managed Pool Administration (`.bot pool`)

*(Administrator / server console)*

Inspect the managed random-bot pool and enroll legacy accounts. See

for the full rebuild procedure.

| Command | Available to | What It Does |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |

- Adoption is the only way a pre-existing hand-made bot account becomes managed. Startup logs a warning listing prefix-matching accounts that are not managed, and those accounts are ignored by the pool until adopted.

- There is deliberately **no** live reset command: rebuilding the pool is a startup operation driven by `AiPlayerbot.RandomBotPoolReset`.

## 7b. Mercenary Contracts at Capital Recruiters (issue #490)

The same six recruiters offer a `Mercenary contracts` branch next to `Hire bots` (and `World buffs`, when enabled) for non-bot level-60 players while `AiPlayerbot.HireGearTiersEnabled = 1`. It tracks the seven hire-gear tier unlock quests (90100-90106): one line per unfinished kill counter (`<label>: x/N, missing: Boss A, Boss B, ...`), opening a sub-page per counter when the roster overflows one gossip line, then the branch's quest list and `< Back`.

Each listed boss counts once per character, ever: kills add up across runs and lockouts. A kill counts when you (or anyone in your group within reward distance — the same rule as normal quest kill credit) land it while the tier's quest is incomplete in your log; bots never take credit. ## 8. Addon Command Transport (`TortoiseBotsManager`)

The `/tbm` UI speaks the same command grammar over a silent transport. While the
server reports the addon channel usable, the addon sends its UI commands as
addon messages instead of `.bot` chat, and the module answers over the same
channel: the request is consumed by the server, no echo reaches nearby players,
and no reply line lands in the chat frame. `.bot` chat stays the transport when
the addon channel is unavailable — ungrouped, a battleground group with no
pre-battleground group, or an older module — and for every hand-typed command.

Typed `.bot` commands and `TBM` addon requests from an in-game player run on the
server's next world tick, before the bots update, in the order the server received
them (issue #833) — that also holds with `AiPlayerbot.Enabled = 0`. Replies come back
in that order on the transport the request used. Received order is not always send
order: party chat and addon messages travel different server paths, so an addon click
can overtake a `/p .bot ...` typed just before it. Chat to bots (`/p stay`,
whispers) and the packets the bots react to share that order with the commands, so
`/p stay` then `/p .bot action follow` ends in follow (issue #846), however close
together you type them: a `.bot` movement order (`.bot action follow/stay/flee/
come/hold/comestay`, the tactical orders, `.bot follow/stay/guard/free`) cancels
the `follow`, `stay`, `guard`, `free`, `flee` and `wander` chat orders the bot has
received but not yet carried out, so the order you typed last wins; packets the AI
handles inline on the world thread (its own casts, hire provisioning) are outside
this ordering and take effect when they arrive. A command whose
sender logs out before that tick is dropped; more than 128 commands from one player
within one tick are ignored with one line `Too many bot commands at once: <n> ignored.`

- Request — addon message prefix `TBM`, body `<verb> [args]` (`action attack`).
- Reply — addon message prefix `TBM`, one line per reply, `TBM:` protocol lines included.
- Verdict — `TBM:TRANSPORT|party` or `TBM:TRANSPORT|none`, trailing each roster response.
- Bot state — `TBM:BOTSTATE_BEGIN|<n>`, one `TBM:BOTSTATE|<bot>|move=<follow|stay|guard|free|flee|custom>,loot=on,aoe=on,autocc=off,savemana=off,boost=on,threat=on,potions=on,mimic=off,spread=off`
  per controllable party bot, then `TBM:BOTSTATE_END`, trailing each roster response. It drives
  the addon's party switches, the Party tab movement label and the bot panel's behaviour tab.
- CC owners — `TBM:CC_ASSIGN_BEGIN|<n>`, one `TBM:CC_ASSIGN|<bot>|<mark>` per controllable party bot that has the `cc` combat strategy and a CC mark set (the same condition the CC hold uses),
  then `TBM:CC_ASSIGN_END`, trailing the roster rows. It is built from the same bot set as BOTSTATE (owned alts and
  hired companions in your party), not from the roster rows, so a hire's `.bot action cc` assignment shows up even
  though the hire is not a roster row. An owned alt that is online but ungrouped is not listed: a CC assignment only
  acts inside a party.
- Capabilities — `TBM:CAPS|pull-seconds,flee,inventory,behavior,claimed-roster`: adjustable pull delays,
  `.bot action flee`, `.bot inv`/`.bot item`, `.bot behavior` with the BOTSTATE stream, and the
  `TBM:CLAIMED_BEGIN|<n>` / `TBM:CLAIMED|<guid>|<name>|<class>|<level>|<state>|<grouped>|<location>|<gearLocked>` /
  `TBM:CLAIMED_END` claimed-guild-bot snapshot (issue #489) for the addon's Guild tab.
  The addon hides the matching controls when a capability is missing.
- Remote commander view — `TBM:VIEW|v1|<seq>|<B|P|H|T|E>|...` frames, sent unprompted to a bound commander whose view is on; grammar in [section 1a](#perception-feed-tbmview-phase-2).
- Version — `TBM:VERSION|<UTC date>-v<N>` trails every roster response, and
  answers `.bot version` and `.bot help` directly. The addon shows it as
  `server <version>` in the `/tbm` window (`server ?` on older servers).

Hand-typed `.bot` commands keep their chat replies, so the CLI and macro surface
is unchanged.

Hazard avoidance preserves accepted waypoint detours in the path sent to movement. If neither side is valid, it does not invent a replacement waypoint.
Talent links accept at most three trees (two `-` separators); malformed extra trees are rejected before parsing.
