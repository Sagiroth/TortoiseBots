# Guild Supply Chain

How guild orders use the guild bank as the crafting store. Each section below is owned by one worker of issue #1384.

## Supply ladder

Each active order tracks its current supply step and item in the character database. Craft orders resolve the first missing material recursively: a known intermediate recipe is considered before farming that intermediate. The counts behind the need list and the ladder come from one whole-tree walk (`GuildOrderReagentTree.h`): every craftable intermediate expands by its guaranteed yield, a base reagent shared by two branches is one row with the summed need, each base reagent is counted three ways: the whole-tree requirement, the bag shortfall, and the shortfall left after bags and mailbox, spent once across the walk (an intermediate in hand shrinks its subtree). A leaf candidate asks each source for the external shortfall instead of one branch's share, while the mailbox leg targets the bag shortfall: the mail only fills what the bags do not cover (the take moves the whole stack a mail carries, so that target sets only the count stored on the order and shown to the player). A mailbox leg the last search could not reach is no stock at all for ten minutes, in the ladder and in the totals alike, and the count a source is asked for is never below the branch's own shortfall. The ladder still decides per branch whether an item is missing; only the counts are whole-tree. A recipe cycle or depth 8 (the header's `kReagentTreeMaxDepth`, shared with the ladder) ends a chain and the item becomes a leaf. For each resulting item the action sequence is bags and mailbox, guild bank, auction house, self gathering/farming/crafting, then `RequestSupply`; an item no source can cover joins the order's need list, and the craft order keeps supplying the rest of the tree.

Bank messages run through `GuildBank::HandleAddonMessages` on the world thread. Map-thread actions enqueue plain bot GUIDs; the world-thread drain re-resolves the order and banker, checks current inventory capacity, and withdraws no more than the order's missing count. A refusal advances the ladder and remains visible as the order reason. Guild gold uses the same queue and core handler, with the order cap and previously withdrawn amount stored on the order row.

The need list is not a waiting-only field: a craft order carries the items only the player can bring while it keeps working, and the ladder skips a listed item like one a running sub-order covers. `needitems` is a waiting state, so its timer and no-progress clock stay stopped, and a working row with no leg of its own (the ladder had nothing left to supply, waiting on running sub-orders) keeps its no-progress clock paused instead of ending while the children work; a row working its own leg keeps every clock, so a stalled leg still ends the round. The store checks bags and mailbox each update and checks the guild bank on a ten-second interval (skipped while the guild bank withdrawal setting is off, or after a bank refusal); the order becomes working again when a listed missing amount can be supplied. A row with a list also re-runs the ladder about every ten minutes (a parked row and a working one alike: a vendor-unlimited item the bot can now afford, a fresh auction listing, or the third-check full retry); a hit on a working row clears the whole list so the ladder re-plans every item, keeps the current step unless it sources a listed item, and one that is still a dead end comes straight back. A dead-end reason (`nosource`, `bankdenied`, `nobanker`) belongs to the leg that produced it and is cleared when a working row takes an item on its list. The whispered need token is persisted as the union of what the player was told: only a new item id or a higher count whispers again, so a list rebuilt one item at a time, a clear-and-replan or a resume stays silent.

The `TBM:ORDER` line keeps its existing fields and appends parent, gold cap, step, need and carry. `<have>` is delivered only (what reached the guild, or the bank stock on a `stocked` row) and keeps its never-drop floor; `<carry>` is the bags-plus-mailbox amount not delivered yet, zero once the row is finished (a list reply reads it from a live bot and otherwise shows the last figure stored on the row), and the two progress counts are what the live updates compare. Both counts come off the row on every update, not only on a scan, so the line after a deposit reports the deposit and the finished row reports no carry. Step, need and progress changes go through the normal world-thread update so list replies and live lines use one formatter.

## Refunds and retry rules

Unspent guild gold (`guildGoldHeld`) is repaid with `DepositMoney` through the same banker path. Finished and cancelled rows park (`cancelled`) instead of disappearing while gold is owed; the refund drain pays from any banker visit and the store clears the row once the gold is back. A new order for the same bot inherits the debt.

Bank table reads are hints: the core commits saves through the async delay queue, so a re-read right after a withdraw or deposit still shows the old value. The item drain reads `guild_bank` once per banker visit, iterates its rows, and treats only the bot's bag-count delta as truth; a failed row skips to the next instead of aborting the visit. The money drain never re-reads: a `WithdrawMoney` the core refuses leaves the bot's money unchanged, which the drain reports as `BankDenied`. An empty bank visit marks the item skipped for ten minutes, and a gold top-up is tried once per auction listing. A leg whose travel search comes back empty three times in a row moves down the ladder the same way (mailbox: skip the mail leg for ten minutes and continue; bank: mark the item skipped and continue with reason `nobanker` when no banker was reachable, `bankdenied` only when the core refused a withdrawal; vendor: next leg), so an unreachable destination never parks the order. The auction leg is the exception: its first empty or rejected auctioneer search moves down the ladder at once (see [Auction leg](#auction-leg)). The auction leg also commits to the first picked city (faction auction house first, else neutral; other cities are filtered out) and counts arrived trips that end with no auctioneer in reach on the leg, so re-picks between cities neither reset the search streak (a leg or item change restarts it, not a landed pick) nor flap the destination; at `AiPlayerbot.GuildOrderAuctionVisits` such visits (default 3, 0 disables) the leg moves down the ladder by itself. The farm leg moves to the next plan zone after three failed searches in one zone (a zone where no source passes the travel gates counts as a failed search); once these moves have gone through every zone of the plan without one landed pick, the leg records `NoSource` and the ladder moves on to a sub-order or the need list instead of cycling the same zones. A craft order's reagent farm also gives up after an hour worked without a new reagent, so the ladder reaches the sub-order before the three-hour no-progress end of the whole order.

## Auction leg

The auction leg is quick on purpose (`GuildOrderAuctionPolicy.h`): one search, a two-minute reachability deadline, and no camping while a better source waits.

- **One search.** A leg whose auctioneer travel search comes back empty or rejected moves down the ladder at once, with the order reason `noauctioneer` ("auctioneer unreachable"). A search that lands at an auctioneer hands off in the same tick when nothing on the counter fits the miss or the budget, with the order reason `nolisting`; the pick is one scan of the listings per visit (`PickAuctionListing`). When the listing's gold top-up was already tried and the bank denied it, the hand-off keeps the bank's reason (`bankdenied`) instead of `nolisting`; `nolisting` stays for a top-up the spending budget cut short. Both causes are written to the step timeline under the leg's own `ah` step, so `.bot order <bot> steps` shows whether the counter was unreachable or had nothing fitting. There is no three-empty-search rule for this leg. A visit keeps buying listings until the miss is covered, then the bags leg collects the mail once and hands back to the ladder through the empty step, so a covered leg never walks back to the counter with a zero count.
- **Reach deadline and travel cap.** Entering the auction leg starts a 20-minute travel cap. The 120 s reach clock starts on arrival instead: the leg's travel target reaching the auctioneer's city, or the bot standing within 100 yd of an auctioneer. If it has not been within interact range of an auctioneer (the core's own 5 yd + line-of-sight check, not the wider 15 yd arrival radius) 120 s after arriving, the leg hands off with `noauctioneer`; a leg that spends its whole 20 minutes walking hands off the same way. An auctioneer coming into reach clears both clocks, so a bot that arrived is never timed out while it searches and a bot still in the field keeps its walk. The arrived-visit rule above stays as a second exit when repeated trips land in a city with no reachable auctioneer.
- **No ping-pong.** A reagent's auction leg is entered at most twice per round. After the first visit the ladder must reach its last source for that reagent (the need stage: the order parks on the item, or a craft order puts it on its need list) before the counter may be tried once more; a leg restart after a failed sub-order or another material does not send the bot back to the auctioneer first. The cycle is memory only (`AuctionLegCycle`) and is cleared by a new round, so a restart starts a fresh pair of visits.
- **Oversized stacks.** The order may buy a listing larger than the miss. Among listings the whole buyout can afford, a covering stack wins only when its whole buyout is no more than filling the miss from the cheapest partial stacks, or, while those cannot fill the miss, when its per-unit price stays within twice the cheapest partial's; with no partial listed at all it wins outright. Among winning covering stacks the smallest wins (least surplus), then the cheapest per unit. The order uses only the miss and the surplus goes back to the auction house.
- **Relist.** Auction winnings arrive by mail, so the sequence is buy -> mailbox (the normal bags leg takes the stack) -> back to the auctioneer, where the same bot posts the surplus before the ladder continues. The relist price per unit is the larger of the bought unit price and the current lowest listing for the item (per-unit prices round up); the duration is the normal 8 hours. A surplus larger than one split packet (255) is posted over several visits. Relists are logged as `GuildAhRelist` (with item, count and buyout) and failures as `GuildAhRelistFailed`.
- **Relist failure.** No auction house access, no free slot for the split, or an unaffordable deposit drops the relist and the surplus stays in the bags. It then takes the order's normal leftover path at delivery: the craft leftover pass (`DeliverCraftLeftovers`, capped by the order's supply gains) deposits it into the guild bank, and a non-craft order's delivery bank pass deposits the stack the same way as farm leftovers. The same drop happens whenever the relist trip hands off without posting (no auctioneer found, the one-search rule, the reach deadline, or a buy-action exit): the pending relist is cleared and the surplus stays in the bags. A relist leg never reads its own item and count as missing supply, so the ladder does not farm, sub-order or need-list what the order already bought. The pending relist is memory only, so a server restart falls back to that same delivery path.
- **Relist ledger.** Every posted relist writes a row to `tortoise_bots_guild_relist` (per bot: guild, item, count, unit price, the posted bid/buyout/deposit and the listing's expiry) because the auction house answers later by mail, often after the order is gone. A sale notification pays its gold into the bot's hands and an expired notification returns the stack. A row is posted, then claimed before its mail is taken: the claim names the mail and the value it promises, and a claim write that fails leaves the mail untouched. Once the take lands the row is received and keeps the value owed to the guild. A claim whose received write failed is settled as received by the next mail read, because the mail is empty or gone and the value is in the bot; a claim whose mail is still there retries the take. A mail is therefore never emptied ahead of the ledger. A mail read settles one row at any order state, or with no order row at all: the take path and the order mail action both scan the auction notifications by subject (`item:random:state`, the core's own `AuctionMailState`) and by the core's sale body (`bidder:bid:buyout:deposit:cut`), so a sale must carry the posting's own buyout and deposit and a bid between its start bid and buyout, while an expired notification needs the posted count and a listing time that has passed. A won-auction mail, the bot's own loot sale, or another auction of the same item is left to normal mail handling, and so is a mail that fits rows of two guilds (logged as ambiguous), so one guild is never credited with another auction's value; look-alike rows of one guild settle as the oldest, because that guild is credited either way. A bank visit runs only when the bot can hand value over now (sale gold in its purse, or the row's returned stack in its bags). A bank trip is at most once per value. Before the banker is asked, the planned value (the gold the bot carries, and bag items up to the row's count) is saved off the row with a checked write; if that write fails, the visit deposits nothing and the row stays. The bank then takes the value through `DepositMoney`/`DepositItem` (the same `GuildBank::HandleAddonMessages` path as the order drains), and whatever it refuses is written back to the row with a checked write. A visit without a banker in reach keeps the row for the next trip, and a row whose guild the bot has since left is dropped with a log line, so no debt is left undrainable. The limit: a crash between saving the planned value and the bank's answer, or a failed write-back after a partial refusal, loses that value instead of paying the guild twice; the log line marks a lost write-back. A posted row is never dropped by age alone: the auction ends 8 hours after posting, but its sale gold or returned stack waits in the bot's mail until the bot logs in, and the core keeps that mail 30 days (`Mail.cpp` `MailDraft::SendMailTo`: `30 * DAY` without COD; the auction mails are sent without an explicit delay). The row is dropped after three days with nothing back only once the bot has been online for an hour after the auction ended (an online bot counts from its login, an offline bot from its `characters.logout_time`), and any unsettled row is dropped with a log line naming the bot and the value once the 30-day mail lifetime after the auction's end has passed (a claimed row is left to the next mail read while its bot is online). A collected (received) row waits for the bank however old. The ledger survives restarts, and a collected relist mail wakes the bot's normal mail read like a buy order's mail does.

## Farm-zone planning

The source planner (`ai/playerbot/GuildOrderSources.cpp`) lists only zones the bot can serve: level and skill gates apply per source, and instance maps are never farm zones. It ranks them by straight-line distance to the zone's nearest sample farm point on the bot's map, zones on the bot's map first, and spawn count and chance only break ties, so a rich zone across the continent never outranks a poorer one next door. The travel pick's plan (map thread only; every other planner caller leaves it off, because the gate touches the node graph and the terrain) also asks the travel layer's own cached route verdict for each same-map zone's sample point (`SharedRouteIsSurvivable`: one node route plus one leg scan, cached five minutes per bot and area cell). A zone that verdict rejects ranks after every zone it accepts, so the nearest zone the bot cannot travel to never outranks a farther one it can. The node graph's only route cost is a full A* per point (`TravelNodeMap::getRoute`), too heavy to run for every zone of every plan, so the cost stays straight-line and reachability comes from that verdict. The pick rules are pure in `runtime/GuildOrderSourcesPolicy.h` (`FarmZoneBefore`, `DropUnreachableFarmZones`, `FarmZoneSkipped`).

When the travel layer's navmesh probe into a farm-leg target reports `incomplete` or `nopath`, `MoveToTravelTargetAction` records that target point for the bot and zone in the order store (`NoteFarmZoneTargetFailed`, memory only, per bot and zone). Failures are kept per target point: a zone is dropped from the plan for 30 minutes (`kFarmZoneUnreachableMs`) only once two different points in it failed inside that window (`kFarmZoneFailedTargets`), because one mesh hole where a single sample spawn sat says nothing about the zone's other farm spots, and the travel pick tries those next. When every zone is dropped the plan stays as it is, because an empty plan reads as `nosource` and would end the order rather than let the travel layer's retry and teleport try again.

## Sub-orders

`RequestSupply(parentBot, item, count)` runs on a bot's map thread: under the store lock it only checks the toggle, the parent row, and an already-running row for the same item in the branch, then records the request in a per-(parent, item) queue slot. The world-thread `Update` drains due requests: it still holds the parent row, enforces the depth limit (`AiPlayerbot.GuildOrderSubOrderDepth`, top-level order counts 1) and the loop guard (same item never twice in one branch, both in `GuildOrderSubOrderPolicy.h`), enumerates guild members by one `guild_member` query per due request, keeps durable module bots (pool-account or claimed, never alts or hires) that are online in the order's guild, and ranks them makers-first through the policy pick. Due requests drain in (parent, item) order starting after the request that used the last tick's planner budget, so one request cannot take every evaluation. The scan is capped at `kSubOrderPlannerEvalsPerTick` planner evaluations per tick: the best free candidate and the busy verdict found so far travel with the request, so a roster larger than the cap is covered over retries and the pick is made only when the walk has completed, ranking every member rather than the current slice. A completed scan with no free candidate but a busy bot that could still make or farm the item keeps the request queued for a fresh scan (a keep holder parked at its stock target never frees up, so it does not count); ten consecutive busy-only scans, or a scan that saw no usable candidate, fail it. A bot that can gather the item itself (skinning, herbalism, mining, fishing source in its farm plan) counts as a farmer even when it knows a recipe, so raw materials are gathered rather than crafted down a reagent chain. An unfinished scan retries about a second later, and the carried candidate is re-checked just before `Give` against the scan's member and busy rules (a stale one restarts the walk); busy candidates and a failed `Give` re-queue on a 45 s timer; terminal failures (no candidate, depth/loop refused, cant, player cancel) all report through `NoteSubOrderFailed`, which merges the item onto the parent's need list. A working parent keeps working and resets its supply leg so the ladder re-plans around the item; a parent already parked in `needitems` just merges (as does any other waiting reason).

A craft order with several missing components delegates them in parallel: the ladder picks the first missing component with no running or queued sub-order for it (`CoveredSubOrderItems`, pure pick `NextUncoveredSubOrderItem` in `GuildOrderSubOrderPolicy.h`), requests it without parking the parent row, and moves on to the next uncovered component on the next refresh, so several sub-orders run at the same time. Each delivered sub-order deposits into the guild bank like a normal order; the parent collects each delivery through its bank leg (`CollectableSubOrderItems` preferred first, `SubOrderDelivered` hand-over once per delivery) and crafts when everything is there. A Sub row for one component never blocks the others: while it waits, the parent keeps supplying (or delegating) the rest. The `TBM:ORDER` line keeps one step field, so it shows the component the parent itself works on while sub-orders show as their own rows.

`GuildOrderRequest` rows in `bot_events.csv` carry `travel, step:item, parent|-, orderItem, type`: one row per supply-leg change, and at most one row a minute while a leg lasts, so a sub-order's short legs are not swallowed by the throttle.

## Vendor trips with full bags

A bot whose bags are held full by its order walks to a vendor only for a sale that frees a bag row or gets it under the 85 % line.

## Bot rank and bank access

Every bot that joins a guild with at least one real player member gets the guild's bot rank so the bots share one rank the master can open bank tabs to; a rank a player later gives a bot by hand stays. All-bot pool guilds are out of scope: they keep their own ranks (including a bot guild master, which is never moved), nobody gives orders there, and their bots keep managing themselves. The whole feature is an on/off switch (`AiPlayerbot.GuildOrderBotRank`, default on); when it is off the server applies no ranks and `TBM:ORDER_ACCESS` carries no rank information (`-|-|-|-|0|0`).

- **Default rank:** the guild's lowest rank (rank count - 1). Guilds without a stored choice use the default; the switch being off disables everything. Rank 0 (guild master) is never moved by any rank path, and an incoming rank 0 (guild creation) is never rewritten on join.
- **Join:** `ClaimGuildAdapter::OnAddMember` rewrites the joining member's rank through `GuildOrders::JoinBotRank` before the core inserts the row, so a bot joins at the bot rank at once. The rule uses one durable bot definition online and offline (claimed into a guild or on a random-pool account, never an autonomous alt-bot); real players' own characters keep the core-assigned rank. The hook runs on the world thread and recomputes the cached management verdict as a side effect (the live joiner counts on top of the stored roster).
- **Startup:** `LoadBotRankManagementAtStartup` only warms the management verdict; it moves no member, so hand-set ranks survive restarts.
- **Bot tab:** `OpenBotBankTabForNextStart` (startup and shutdown, before the core's final bank save) raises `guild_bank_tabs.minrank1` to the bot rank where it is lower. The core loads tab ranks into memory once and its in-place reload would duplicate bank items, so the write applies from the next start; no core seam.
- **Scope:** `GuildHasRealPlayerMember(guildId, excludeGuidLow)` decides from the database on the world thread only — join, leave, rank command, startup. The exclude parameter covers the remove hook, where the leaver's row is already gone from the in-memory roster but its database delete is still queued. AI ticks never run this query.
- **Change:** `.bot order rank <rankIndex>` (promote+demote right required) stores the choice in `tortoise_bots_guild_bot_rank` (always a real rank 1..lowest) and moves every bot via `ApplyBotRankToGuild`, which reads the roster once for both the scope check and the moves and refreshes the verdict.
- **Management gate:** `GuildOrders::BotRankBlocksManagement` is a cache read only (`BotRankStore`, unknown means not blocked), so map-thread AI ticks never touch the roster or the database. The world thread keeps it warm: join/leave hooks, rank command, disband, and the startup sweep. It is true exactly when the guild has a real player member and the bot rank is active. The four guild management actions (invite, promote, demote, remove) report not-useful there, as does `guild manage nearby` (which also returns early from `Execute`).
- **Access line:** `GuildOrders::FormatAccessLine` reads the tab minimum ranks from `guild_bank_tabs` (the same table the core bank loads, so no new seam) and computes `TBM:ORDER_ACCESS` from the pure policy in `runtime/GuildOrderAccessPolicy.h`. No bank row means no tabs and no gold. The gold bit follows the core money rule (only ranks 0-1 may withdraw) once a bank exists. The red header rule is the contract: any denied tab, or no gold.

## Step history

Each order keeps a bounded, memory-only timeline of its own step changes: one entry per change of the tuple `(state, reason, supplyStep, supplyItemId)` that `TBM:ORDER` already shows (state word, reason label, step label, leg item), newest last. The store records it at the single point where all mutators have settled, the end of `OrderStore::Tick`, and holds up to `kOrderStepHistoryMax = 16` entries per order, dropping the oldest past that (counted by a saturating `dropped`). Time is read only when an entry is appended, so a pass with no change costs one tuple compare.

It is never persisted (no schema, no row write) and never pushed live: history starts empty after a server restart and a new order, a reassign or a reload starts a fresh one. `.bot order <bot> steps` reads it on demand, read-only like `order list`, and answers oldest first with `TBM:ORDER_STEPS_BEGIN|<bot>|<n>|<dropped>`, one `TBM:ORDER_STEP|<bot>|<ageSec>|<state>|<reason>|<step>|<itemId>` per entry (`ageSec` = now minus the entry time, clamped at 0), and `TBM:ORDER_STEPS_END|<bot>`. `TBM:ORDER` is unchanged; an order-less or cancelled bot answers with the empty pair. The capability token `guild-order-steps` is sent next to `guild-orders`.

A relist ledger settlement is not a step: collecting its mail or paying the bank changes no `(state, reason, supplyStep, supplyItemId)` tuple, so it adds no timeline entry and leaves a finished order's history exactly as it ended. The ledger row and its bank deposit are the record of that value.
