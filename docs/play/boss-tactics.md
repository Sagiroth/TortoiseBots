# Boss Tactics Table

> Work in progress: this feature is still being built and tested.

A boss fight has a few mechanics that generic combat cannot know: which spell to interrupt, which add to kill first, where not to stand. This page describes the data table that holds them and the strategy that applies them.

It is **off by default**. A normal player's bots play exactly as before.

## When it is active

The `boss tactics` strategy is added to a bot by the `enter boss tactics` trigger of the `dungeon` strategy (re-checked every 5 ticks) when all of these hold:

- the bot is on a map that has a row in `ai_boss_tactic` OR `ai_boss_stand` (a stand set alone arms it), and
- either `AiPlayerbot.DungeonTactics = 1` (server owner opt-in; default `0`), or the bot is in a bound group:
  - a remote bind needs `AiPlayerbot.RemoteCommander = 1`,
  - an `inparty` bind needs `AiPlayerbot.DungeonSelfClear = 1`.

`leave boss tactics` removes it when the bot is on a map without rows (not while teleporting or loading), or when the key is off and the bot is no longer in a bound group. The gate is `BossTacticsWanted`.

A boss that vanishes mid-fight (invisible and off the bot's attackers list while alive and in combat, like Arlokk) keeps its rows for up to 90 s as long as the bot still has attackers and the boss stays within 60 yd and out of evade mode (`BossPresenceMemory`), so rows for its adds, such as the Zulian Prowler `kill_first`, stay active during the vanish. The rows end with the fight, the boss's death or evade, or the grace window.

A boss the raid is fighting also stays in every raid bot's fight for 10 s after the last bot saw it in its attacker list, once `AiPlayerbot.BossTacticsKeepsEngagedBoss = 1` (default `0`, recommended on for Onyxia's Lair; not yet tested in other raids): the bots that see the boss note it in one registry, and a bot that lost it (a melee bot that cannot reach an airborne boss gives up on it) reads it back while the boss is alive, in combat, not evading and within 150 yd, and while the bot itself is in combat. Its rows and its stand seats stay active for it.

Commander-bound groups use it because commanders cannot give interrupt and kill orders fast enough. The key stays `0` because it changes how bots play.

Two extra opt-ins:

- `linked_death` needs `AiPlayerbot.DungeonRunLinkedDeath = 1` (default `0`). Rows exist but are unread while it is off.
- Aura rows need `AiPlayerbot.DungeonRunAuraTactics = 1` (default `0`). These are `dps_hold_after`, `dispel_priority`, `flee_on_aura`, `freeze_on_aura`, `taunt_after_cast`, `swap_on_aura`.
- An `avoid_cast` row in mode `all` needs `AiPlayerbot.DungeonRunAvoidCastAll = 1` (default `0`): with it off the row holds a tank the caster is on like a default row.
- `gate_hold` needs `AiPlayerbot.DungeonRunGateHold = 1` (default `0`, recommended on). Rows exist but are unread while it is off.
- `tank_post` rows need `AiPlayerbot.DungeonRunTankPost = 1` (default `0`, recommended on): a named tank waits at a spot for a unit that is not in the fight yet, then attacks it on arrival.
- The raid guard leaves an assigned unit to its tank with `AiPlayerbot.DungeonRunGuardLeavesAssignedTank = 1` (default `0`, recommended on): its off-tank pick-ups never send another tank at a unit an `until_tank` row with a tank slot or a `tank_post` row assigns, while the assigning kind's own key is on (`AiPlayerbot.DungeonRunAuraTactics` for `until_tank`, `AiPlayerbot.DungeonRunTankPost` for `tank_post`; with the key off the row does nothing and the unit is taken like any other add). Each kind keeps its own slot, so the enabled kind's tank is checked (the lower slot when both are on), and that tank keeps the unit for 8 s from the tick the unit first went loose: an out-of-reach, polymorphed unit, a fight with no free off-tank, or a tick the healer guard or the main tank's join takes does not restart that clock. The tank's selected target is no hold, a charmed tank holds nothing, and a dead tank leaves the raid tank order so the next tank inherits the slot; past that grace the guard takes the unit.
- A `tank_post` row in mode `hold_spot` needs `AiPlayerbot.DungeonRunTankPostHoldSpot = 1` (default `0`, recommended on): the post starts while the tank holds another hostile, its arrival latches for that unit from the first arrived tick (even when that tick drags the unit back or yields to a seat), it drags the arrived unit back to the spot and it yields to a boss stand seat; with it off every `tank_post` row is a plain post.
- The `hold_spot` leash needs `AiPlayerbot.DungeonRunTankPostLeash = 1` (default `0`, recommended on, needs the two keys above): before the awaited unit arrives, a tank more than 8 yd from the spot walks back to it keeping its current target, instead of following a whelp away; with it off the tank keeps the other hostile and the post waits.
- `.reload config` re-reads these keys without a restart.

## The table

`ai_boss_tactic` is created and filled by `data/sql/world/*_world.sql` migrations. It is read at startup into a cache, one pass, nothing per tick.

- `ai_boss_tactic` is also re-read by `.bot run reloadplans` (no restart needed for it).
- `ai_boss_stand` is startup-only (restart needed).

| Column | Meaning |
| :--- | :--- |
| `map` | Map id. |
| `boss_entry` | Creature entry whose presence among the bots' attackers activates the row. |
| `kind` | See Kinds below: `interrupt`, `kill_first`, `behind`, `avoid_cast`, `tank_hold`, `hold_kill`, `one_at_a_time`, `linked_death`, `seek_aura`, `seek_pull`, `pull_gate`, `spread`, `use_object`, `threat_successor`, `anchor`, `tank_ignore`, `gate_hold`, `tank_post`, plus the aura kinds behind their own key (`dps_hold_after`, `dispel_priority`, `flee_on_aura`, `freeze_on_aura`, `taunt_after_cast`, `swap_on_aura`). |
| `target_entry` | The creature the row is about; `0` means the boss itself. `hold_kill` is inverted: `boss_entry` is the spared entry and `target_entry` is the blocker; `gate_hold` uses the same inversion, and its blocker must be a different unit from the spared one. `one_at_a_time` uses target `0` and `boss_entry` is the paced entry. `linked_death` lists one partner per row and `boss_entry` is the anchor. A `seek_aura` row names the source creature whose death leaves the cloud; a `seek_pull` row names that same seek source; a `tank_post` row names the unit the post waits for; a `pull_gate` row has `target_entry` 0 and names the boss itself, as does a `spread` row. |
| `spell_id` | `interrupt` / `avoid_cast` spell. `seek_aura`: the aura a bot must carry; `pull_gate`: the aura the boss must not wear; `spread`: the splash the spacing answers (documents the row only). |
| `priority` | `kill_first`: lower dies first. `hold_kill`: budget, how many blockers may live before DPS stop (`0` = not one more). `gate_hold`: how many blockers may still be fighting (`0` = not one more). `linked_death`: group id. `seek_aura`: the health % at which DPS stop on the source while the gather is not ready; `pull_gate`: most stacks allowed (`0` = none); `taunt_after_cast` with mode `until_tank`: which raid tank takes the subject. `anchor`: the tanks' radius in yards (`0`: the shared `param`). `seek_pull`: the most walk-outs started in one seek cycle (1-5). `tank_post`: the raid tank slot that holds the post (1-7). |
| `param` | `behind`: yards of front kept clear. `avoid_cast`: yards left. `gate_hold`: health gate, 1-99 % (`hp_min`/`hp_max` are refused). `linked_death`: hold percent, 0-100 exclusive of 0. `dps_hold_after`: hold seconds, 0-30. `seek_aura`: gather radius around the source; `pull_gate`: seconds the run leader holds before ending the run (1-300); `spread`: yards the ranged raid keeps apart (5-30); `taunt_after_cast`: window seconds (0 means 10). `anchor`: the circle's radius in yards for everyone, 10-60. `seek_pull`: the kill lead in seconds, 10-85 (the forced burn of an engaged source starts this long before the cycle spell is due). `tank_post`: the arrival radius in yards, 5-40 (the awaited unit has arrived within this of the spot). |
| `tactic_mode` | Only some kinds read it. `until_tank`: a `taunt_after_cast` row assigns a tank by raid order while the subject stays off a tank, and opens a new window every `param` seconds until a tank has it. `all`: an `avoid_cast` row clears the tanks too while the listed spell is cast (needs `AiPlayerbot.DungeonRunAvoidCastAll = 1`), instead of letting a tank the caster is on hold it. `strict`: a `threat_successor` row opts its boss into `AiPlayerbot.DungeonRunThreatSuccessorStrict`; refused on every other kind. `hold_spot`: a `tank_post` row starts the post while the tank holds another hostile, latches the arrival, drags the arrived unit back to the spot and yields to a boss stand seat (needs `AiPlayerbot.DungeonRunTankPostHoldSpot = 1`); refused on every other kind. |
| `window_s` | `gate_hold`: blocker search radius in yards (`0` = 200, else 50-250). `linked_death`: burn window seconds (default 7). `seek_aura`: dose window opens this many seconds before the cycle is due. `seek_pull`: the last start in seconds, 10-85 (no walk-out starts with less left to the due time). `tank_post`: the longest hold in seconds, 10-120. `0` for every other kind. |
| `cycle_spell`, `cycle_s` | `seek_aura` only: boss spell whose aura restarts the cycle clock, and the cycle length in seconds. `0` for every other kind. |
| `spot_x`, `spot_y`, `spot_z` | `seek_aura`: where the source is held and killed. `anchor`: the circle's centre (the boss's home). `tank_post`: the spot the tank waits at. `0` for every other kind. |
| `burn_order` | `linked_death` only: kill order once released. First non-empty wins. |
| `hp_min` / `hp_max` / `bot_aura` / `mode` | Row gates, where read (never on `seek_aura`, `pull_gate`, `spread` or `anchor`). |
| `comment` | Free text. |

The true primary key is `(map, boss_entry, kind, target_entry, spell_id, hp_min, hp_max)`.

The loader skips unusable rows and logs the count: unknown kind, interrupt without spell, behind without radius, avoid without spell or radius, tank_hold with target_entry, hold_kill without target_entry, one_at_a_time with target_entry, linked_death without target_entry or param outside (0,100], `seek_aura` without target_entry, spell_id, cycle_spell or spot, param outside (0,10], priority above 99, cycle_s outside (0,600] or window_s outside (0,cycle_s), `pull_gate` with target_entry, without spell_id or param outside (0,300], `spread` with target_entry or param outside 5-30, `threat_successor` with target_entry or a gate, `anchor` with target_entry, spell_id or a gate, without a spot, param outside 10-60 or priority above param, `gate_hold` without target_entry, with its own boss as the blocker, or with a band, spell, mode or spot, param outside 1-99 or window_s neither 0 nor 50-250, `seek_pull` without target_entry, with a spell, a spot, cycle columns, a gate or a mode word, priority outside 1-5 or param/window_s outside 10-85, `tank_post` without target_entry, a spot, priority outside 1-7, param outside 5-40, window_s outside 10-120 or with a spell or a mode word other than `hold_spot`, a cycle/spot/mode column the kind does not read, duplicate key.

### Kinds

| Kind | What bots do | Who |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| `interrupt` | A bot with a ready interrupt in range uses it: Pummel, Shield Bash, Kick, Counterspell, Earth Shock, first castable wins. Class interrupts on the current target are unchanged. Generic slot then channel slot, wider radius wins. | Any bot with an interrupt |
| `kill_first` | Takes the listed creature before anything else. Lowest priority first, then a creature whose victim is a healer of ours (a Shade of Jin'do on a healer comes before one on a damage dealer), nearer breaks the rest of the tie, nothing past 40 yd. Generic assist and AoE do not pull back. An explicit attack order wins, with one exception for the run's own orders: a bot carrying a row's `bot_aura` (Delusions 24306) turns on the nearest live unit of that row within 40 yd (the same reach for melee and ranged) even under the run's focus or attack order, which it drops; the run's focus skips it while that unit lives within 40 yd, never sends it at a totem, and once the aura is gone it returns to normal targeting. A bot that does not switch while its subject is in reach logs `BossTacticsKillFirst` with the reason and its current target, once per 5 s. A needs-aura subject takes direct damage only, so no DoT is cast at it and no pet is sent at it. A player's attack or focus order is never overridden. | DPS only, never tanks or healers |
| `hold_kill` | DPS leave `boss_entry` alone while more than `priority` living blockers stand within 50 yd. Pickers skip the held entry. A bot already on it takes the `kill_first` pick if there is one, else the nearest attackable within 40 yd, else stops. The grid search counts a fighting or blocked creature, so the portal counts while an unwoken add does not. | DPS |
| `gate_hold` | DPS leave `boss_entry` alone while its health is at or below `param` % (1-99) and more than `priority` units of `target_entry` within `window_s` yd (0 = 200) are alive, in combat and not evading. The blocker must be a different unit from the spared one. An evaded or out-of-combat blocker never counts, so an unkillable second boss does not stall the spared one's kill. The pickers, the tank's-victim assist and the raid mark drop the spared unit, and an area cast or DoT within 10 yd of it is stood down; tanks and healers keep their own pick and an explicit attack order wins. Needs `AiPlayerbot.DungeonRunGateHold = 1` (default `0`, recommended on). | free DPS |
| `one_at_a_time` | All DPS kill one living unit of the entry at a time: lowest-health fighting unit, lowest guid breaks ties. CC and evading units are never picked. | DPS |
| `linked_death` | The group must die together. Free DPS take the highest-health-fraction living member within 50 yd. A member at or below `param` % is held: never picked, AoE leaves it out, area and DoT casts are vetoed. Burn order applies once released. Release latches with `window_s` once armed (5 s, default 7 s); evade resets it. | Free DPS |
| `behind` | Within `param` yd in the front half, step 120 degrees off the boss facing at the same distance. | Everyone except tanks and the bot the boss attacks |
| `avoid_cast` | Within `param` yd 3D of the caster (channel slot included), walk to 2 yd past the radius on ring points, shortest walk first. Listed blasts hold reach, chase, heal, follow, and stay-back moves until the cast ends or the caster dies. A row in mode `all` also clears the tanks while its spell is cast (no tank holds it); with `AiPlayerbot.DungeonRunAvoidCastAll = 0` the row holds like a default row. | Everyone the row covers (tanks included for `all`) |
| `tank_hold` | Opt-in per caster, `target_entry` 0. A tank whose target is that caster stays in the blast, keeps it, and is the preferred pickup. Only its own caster's blast, and only while that caster is on a non-tank; the pickup outranks the raid mark when nearer. | Tanks |
| `dps_hold_after` * | Hold DPS after the trigger, `param` seconds. | DPS; needs `DungeonRunAuraTactics = 1` |
| `dispel_priority` * | Ranks aura removal. | Dispensers; needs `DungeonRunAuraTactics = 1` |
| `flee_on_aura` * | The carrier leaves the group. | Carrier; needs `DungeonRunAuraTactics = 1` |
| `freeze_on_aura` * | The carrier holds all actions but freeze. | Carrier; needs `DungeonRunAuraTactics = 1` |
| `taunt_after_cast` * | Taunt on the trigger. Mode `until_tank`: while the subject stands on a non-tank, the row's `priority` tank by raid order taunts it back, and a new window opens every `param` seconds for as long as it stays off a tank. One tank holds a window until it ends or that tank can no longer take a taunt back (death, a charm, a stun, a hex, a fear, a silence, a polymorph); its taunt being on cooldown after its own cast never hands the window on, so one window burns one taunt; a tank that can take the taunt now (in melee of the subject, targeting it, not its victim, in control, not silenced or polymorphed, its taunt off cooldown) is picked before one that can cast its taunt at all, and that one before a tank that cannot cast now, so a Hexed tank never takes a window from a healthy one; a taunt that was cast and still failed hands the next window to the next such tank. `spell_id` 0 is legal for instant charms. A row keyed to an instant teleport (`spell_id` 24466, Jin'do's Banish) also opens when the subject's tank victim teleports: the spell leaves no aura and no cast to read, so the trigger is the tank landing at the spell's destination (or its position jumping more than 20 yd between two reads). The window is latched while the subject follows the tank, and closes as regained when another tank has the subject, the teleported tank is back within 10 yd of it, or after 12 s. A tank that merely stands far away opens nothing. Only mode `until_tank` does anything: a `blink` row, or a spell-keyed row in any other mode, is accepted by the loader and ignored. A creature-charmed tank only leaves the order while `AiPlayerbot.DungeonRunCharmYield = 1` is also on. A subject the game makes immune to taunts opens no episode at all: no taunt of that tank can land on it, so it keeps its attack and its taunt for a target that can be moved, and the server log says `taunt_after skipped: target taunt-immune` once per fight for that subject. With `AiPlayerbot.DungeonRunGuardLeavesAssignedTank = 1` the raid guard sends no other tank at the subject either: the window alone moves it between tanks. | Tanks; needs `DungeonRunAuraTactics = 1` |
| `swap_on_aura` * | Tank rotation. | Tanks; needs `DungeonRunAuraTactics = 1` |
| `seek_aura` | The raid doses itself from a dying source before the boss's cycle spell lands. The first siphon is timed from the fight's combat start, so cycle 1 gets the same dose window as every later one. While the dose window is open (`window_s` seconds before due, and up to 8 seconds past it when a sighting is missed, after which the cycle estimate rolls forward one cycle at a time (the grace stays under the gap between a cycle and its window, so the rolled cycle's window opens soon after); with `DungeonRunSeekCycleRoll` the still-missed sighting also advances the fight's cycle number, so the walk-out's spent cycle reopens)), bots lacking `spell_id` walk to the living source and stand within `param` yd; DPS bring it to `priority` % and stop until the gather is ready, then burn it so the cloud lands on the raid. A Son that is not fighting yet is fetched for real: the second off-tank walks to it (moving itself, not only setting a target) and hits it until it joins the fight, then drags it to the spot, and it keeps after that Son while the Son stays within 90 yd of the spot and for up to 20 seconds even after it leaves the 60 yd pickup radius; with fewer than three tanks alive (exactly two) the raid's nearest hunter (else a ranged damage dealer) opens a Son within 35 yd of the spot with one shot from its ranged weapon (the auto shot or a wand, never a damage over time whose ticks would run through the hold) and the first off-tank takes it over; a dead, charmed or boss-targeted member is never that puller. The source search reaches the spot's radius while three tanks are there, and 35 yd once the burn is forced, a source inside that reach already fights at or below the hold percent, or any source inside it is already fighting: that source becomes the gather and the kill pick wherever it stands. The only held hit that is let through is the puller's own strike on the Son its pull fetched, from the pull's start until that Son is dragged in (a DoT is still vetoed there). A Son that only joins the fight with the siphon due within 30 seconds is burned at once instead of waiting for the forced burn. A Son that fights a healer or another non-tank for 3 seconds is taunted off by the first off-tank, whatever the third off-tank does. With `AiPlayerbot.DungeonRunSeekNearestTake = 1` and a live `anchor` row the first off-tank does not wait the 3 seconds when the Son is not the open meet's, its victim stands inside the raid circle (plus 2 yd), the third off-tank is more than 8 yd from that Son or its taunt is not ready, and the first off-tank's taunt is ready within 8 yd (it is not the boss's victim and holds no Son); the third off-tank then leaves a fighting Son on the first off-tank (not one on the main tank); the main tank never takes; without a live anchor the 3 s rule applies. Only the picked source may be damaged, and only inside the window — every other Son stays held, and with the window shut the pick is left at whatever health it has so it serves the next siphon. An area cast that reaches a held Son is vetoed even when the picked Son beside it may burn, a Son that is not fighting is never picked, and once the whole raid carries the aura nothing is killed until the next siphon strips it. A damage-over-time with no direct part (Corruption, Serpent Sting) and a channelled one (Mind Flay, Drain Life) never goes on a Son, since its ticks would run through the hold; a nuke that also ticks (Fireball, Immolate, Moonfire) is allowed only on the Son the hold lets burn. The off-tank holding the Son stops auto-attacking it at or below the hold percent plus 10 while the window is shut, and keeps the threat. Hunters keep the siphon cycle: the pet is dismissed from 5 s before the due time until 2 s after it (and while the siphon's aura is on the bot) and called back afterwards, because an undosed pet is a full heal of its own. A warlock dismisses its demon once when Hakkar is engaged and summons nothing during the fight — a new demon costs a soul shard and a 10 s cast — and never an Infernal or a Doomguard. A shaman unsummons its totems for the same window and places none, since each totem is one more undosed unit. Needs `AiPlayerbot.DungeonRunSeekAura = 1` (default `0`); main tank and first off-tank never seek. | every bot without the aura except the two tanks; second off-tank pulls and holds the source (a hunter pulls when it is not there); pet owners send the pet away |
| `seek_pull` | The seek walk-out's per-cycle rules for one `seek_aura` source (walk 51). With `AiPlayerbot.DungeonRunSeekPullCycle = 1` and a row for the fight: each Son is walked again in every cycle (the walked list is per cycle), up to `priority` walk-outs (1-5) start a cycle while the siphon is at least `window_s` seconds away, a failed walk-out reopens the cycle (a puller-side abort frees its Son and leaves the puller out of the picks for 60 s; a no-stand abort, a vanished Son and a timeout keep the Son for the cycle), the stand search fans 10 and 20 degrees either side and falls back to a hunter's 33 yd (hunter only) when nothing fits at 28, a puller that stands at its stand 6 s without landing the shot aborts `no shot`, the pick takes any ranged damage dealer on the platform (60 yd of the anchor's centre, at or above z 46.5) even mid-cast or on a cooldown, the puller faces the Son every tick while in shot position, the no-shot clock keeps running through stand moves when the puller is already in shot position, no walk-out starts before the fight is 19 s old (the first Cause Insanity), and starts go from the seek_aura row's window (80 s for Hakkar) down to the 60 s last start; an engaged Son is burned from `param` seconds before the siphon. Hakkar's row uses a 75 s lead: a Son killed any time from 11 s after one siphon still doses the next (Poisonous Blood lasts 90 s and the siphon strips it only at its own 8 s aura's end), so the burn starts as soon as the pulled Son is engaged. The forced burn also skips the 4 yd gather, so the cloud lands where the receiving off-tank takes the Son, inside the tanks' circle; the walk measures `raid_in_cloud` as its guardrail. Documented limits: starts are allowed only from the seek row's window down to 60 s before the siphon (50 s with `DungeonRunSeekSiphonClock`), so the retries mostly serve fast threat aborts and a no-shot or no-stand abort rarely leaves time for a second walk-out (the siphon clock's 15 s no-sight cut is what reopens them); a missed siphon sighting rolls the due time but not the cycle number (with `DungeonRunSeekCycleRoll` it rolls the cycle number too, so the walked list and the spent cycle clear), so by default the walked list is not cleared until the next sighting; from 45 s the forced burn also marks the gather ready, so an engaged picked Son dies without waiting for the 4 yd gather; a stand that keeps jumping more than 3 yd while the puller is not in shot position can keep resetting the no-shot clock, which the 25 s timeout then catches; the `!s.shot` guard also covers timeouts (untested); the fight has one walk-out slot and a failed puller's walk back holds it, so a failed walk-out rarely leaves time for a retry (12 of walk 52's 148 starts were retries); the cycle-1 gate is a fixed 19 s, not a sighting of the first charm (the charm landed at +17-18 s in 55 of 59 fights, at +38-40 s in the other 3). `target_entry` the seek source, `spell_id` 0, no spot, cycle columns, gate or mode word. Read straight from the table by the walk-out. Needs `AiPlayerbot.DungeonRunSeekAura` and `AiPlayerbot.DungeonRunSeekPullCycle`. | Ranged walk-out pullers |
| `pull_gate` | A run-leader gate, not a fight scan: while the boss wears `spell_id` above `priority` stacks the run leader never pulls it and notes the gate; after `param` seconds it ends the run. Needs `AiPlayerbot.DungeonRunPullGate = 1` (default `0`). | the run leader |
| `spread` | While the boss fights, the ranged raid keeps `param` yd apart (the spell only documents the splash); a bot on a stay spreads too while `AiPlayerbot.RaidReactions = 1` (the universal raid spread no longer exempts a stay then; with it off the action still refuses a stay-held bot). Needs `AiPlayerbot.DungeonRunBossSpread = 1` (default `0`). With `AiPlayerbot.DungeonRunBossSpreadSlots = 1` (default `0`, recommended on) each ranged bot and healer instead owns one point on rings of 6 and 12 slots around the boss anchor, `param` yd apart, and stands on it whenever the seek does not need it, so the legacy step-away defers to that slot hold. | ranged bots and healers; melee and tanks never spread |
| `needs_aura` | Only a bot carrying `spell_id` may target the unit; with `AiPlayerbot.DungeonRunAuraGatedTargets = 1` other bots leave it alone. | Any bot with the aura |
| `use_object` | One claimed bot clicks the boss's game object at the right moment; mode `tank_anchor` also walks the boss over to it. Needs `AiPlayerbot.DungeonRunUseObject = 1` (default `0`). | Claimed clicker (free damage, healer or non-tank) |
| `burn_mana` | A few eligible bots (no tank, no healer, a Mana Burn priest, a Drain Mana warlock or a Viper Sting hunter; `priority` of them, `0` = all, ordered by guid) drain the boss's mana while it stands at or above the row's start % (`param`); only members that run the boss tactics and can cast right now (in combat, in control, their spell off cooldown, the boss in their spell's own range, enough mana) take a slot, a global cooldown does not cost one, and the burn pauses while the boss wears the stone-phase aura 25685. `target_entry` is 0, `spell_id` is unused. Needs `AiPlayerbot.DungeonRunBurnMana = 1` (default `0`). | Eligible drainers; the rest of the raid keeps its rotation |
| `threat_successor` | The boss itself (`target_entry` 0): its damage dealers are measured against the highest threat among the assigned off-tanks instead of the main tank, so the off-tank stays second and a threat reset (Hakkar's Cause Insanity) hands the boss to it instead of to a damage dealer. An off-tank counts as the reference once it carries more than a tiny absolute amount (1 % of the highest tank's threat, or 100 threat when that is larger), so a barely engaged or just-reset off-tank cannot stop the damage while one that is merely behind on damage still takes the boss after a threat reset. Only the boss's own data turns this on; most raid bosses are taunt-immune but keep the highest tank as the reference. Read straight from the table, so the kind takes no gate. ## Jin'do the Hexxer (map 309, entry 11380)

See the Zul'Gurub table above for his rows. Delusions of Jin'do 24306 picks the carriers: only a carrier can hurt a Shade of Jin'do 14986, and only a carrier targets it.

### Carrier strike

Behind `AiPlayerbot.DungeonRunCarrierStrike` (default `0`): a cursed healer or off-tank turns on its own Shade while the raid can spare it, striking just below a critical heal so real healing always wins. Damage dealers never strike (the kill-first row already sends them at Shades), and the main tank never leaves the boss.

The strike is refused when the bot carries no Delusions, when no Shade stands within 40 yd, when an off-tank holds the boss, or on any fight whose boss has a `threat_successor` row: leaving a boss that cannot be taunted would drop the damage dealers under half the main tank's threat and hand him to one on the next threat wipe, so no off-tank strikes there (except for a Shade on a healer under `AiPlayerbot.DungeonRunShadeHealerCover`, see [Shade healer cover](#shade-healer-cover)). A healer also stays on the raid while a `now` dispel waits that it could cure, while the lowest member in heal reach is under the low-health line (`AiPlayerbot.LowHealth`, default 50 %), while the main tank is under 60 %, while fewer than 2 other healers that can heal the raid are standing, or while it is under 30 % mana; the lowest-member and main-tank lines hold an off-tank too. A healer on another floor does not count as cover: more than 6 yd of height difference (down in the troll pit, or up on the room floor while the carrier is in the pit) puts it out of the raid's healing, as do death, the curse itself, or a charm.

### Finding the Shade

Behind `AiPlayerbot.DungeonRunCarrierScan` (default `0`): the Shades are summoned on a carrier and only a carrier can see them, so a carrier's attackers list can miss the Shade standing beside it (walk 53: 65 % of Shade-frames had no cursed bot attacking the Shade, and 295 of 357 kill-first no-switch rows said only `not_read`). With the key on, a carrier (a free DPS or one under the run's own order) reads the Shade's entry straight off the nearby grid and takes the nearest one that passes every check, in this order: a living Shade of that entry is there (`no_unit` when none is), it belongs to this fight (`ineligible`), it stands within 40 yd (`beyond_reach`), the bot can see it (`no_los`), and the bot may attack it (`invalid_target`). The checks are the same rules the ordinary pick uses, so the scan never widens what a carrier may attack, and a player's own attack or focus order is never overridden (`player_order`). The scan runs before the ordinary pick is accepted: a free DPS takes every open `kill_first` row, so a Powerful Healing Ward or a Brain Wash Totem in view would otherwise win the pick while a Shade lives, and a carrier is the only one who can kill that Shade (the uncursed raid is left the ward and the totem). A Shade the scan finds therefore replaces a ward or totem pick; with no Shade found, or for a bot without the curse, the ordinary pick stands, and an ordinary pick that already is a Shade is kept as it is.

The `BossTacticsKillFirst` row then carries the word above, or `not_pickable` when a Shade is in the fight and not attackable at all, and `attack_refused` when the attack on any carrier pick was refused anyway (the scan's or the ordinary one); it also appends `codes=<word>:<n>|...` counts (`none:<n>` counts the hits that passed every check), `grid=<hits>`, `pickable=<0|1>` and `mode=<carrier|free|none>`, so a no-switch row no longer needs a frame walk to explain itself. When the scan's Shade replaces an ordinary pick, one `BossTacticsKillFirst` row per bot per 5 s says `reason=scan_over` with `pick=<entry>:<guid>,over=<entry of the replaced pick>,mode=<carrier|free>`. With the key off the pick and the rows are exactly as before.

With the key on, the grid-found Shades are also seen by the two other readers of the carrier pick: the carrier-strike pick (`FindBossCarrierStrikePick`, which a cursed healer or off-tank uses) and the Commander's focus skip (`Commander.cpp`, which leaves the run's focus alone while a carrier has a Shade to hit).

### The carrier hold

Behind `AiPlayerbot.DungeonRunCarrierHold` (default `0`): a Delusions carrier standing on its Shade keeps it. The "not dps target active" trigger fires whenever the bot's current target differs from its "dps target" (the skull, Jin'do), and its action "dps assist" (relevance 60) outranks kill-first (21), so a carrier was pulled off a live Shade for the boss (76 times in walk 54, 65 in walk 53). With the key on the trigger stays quiet when the current target is a needs_aura subject the bot carries the aura for (`CarrierHoldsCurrent`); a bot without the aura on the Shade, a carrier on any other target, and every bot with the key off keep today's rule. The kill-first trigger is also checked every second under the key, whatever `DungeonRunTotemFocus` says, so the re-pick after a Shade dies no longer waits up to two seconds (median 2.0 s before the key). Needs `DungeonRunAuraTactics` for the needs_aura rows.

Not recommended on: CarrierScan is redundant while KillFirstGrid is on, because the ordinary grid pass reads the same kill-first units and the Shade's kill_first row has priority 0, which beats the ward and the totem (walk 54: every `no_unit` row was an honest "no Shade within 40 yd"). The scan stays in the code; run with `DungeonRunCarrierScan` off.

### The taunt gate

Behind `AiPlayerbot.DungeonRunTauntGate` (default `0`): a tank does not taunt a Shade of Jin'do (only a carrier may damage it, and a taunt only pulls its Shadow Shock onto a second tank) or a creature the core marks immune to taunts (Jin'do, where a taunt moves the tank's threat onto a damage dealer). Every single-target taunt is gated: the warrior's Taunt (the taunt row, the snare-target and off-target forms) and Mocking Blow, the druid's Growl, the paladin's Hand of Reckoning, the shaman's Earthshaker Slam (each with its on-attacker form), and the boss actions that cast a class taunt themselves (the tank swap, the seek take, the taunt-after window). A refusal logs `BossTacticsTauntGate` with `reason=needs_aura` (the bot lacks the row's aura), `reason=holding_boss` (a Delusions carrier that is the boss's current victim, the main tank holding Jin'do: a Shade taunt there moved the boss's threat off it, 19 of 114 Shade taunts in walk 54; a cursed off-tank that does not hold the boss may still taunt the Shade) or `reason=taunt_immune`, at most once per bot and reason per 5 s (a `taunt_immune` row no longer hides a `needs_aura` row in the same window). The gate also drops a needs_aura unit (the Shade) from the "attacker of non-tank target" scan, which feeds both the taunt actions and the warrior gap closer's Charge: an uncursed warrior cannot hurt a Shade either (a tank that carries Delusions keeps the Shade in that scan and may taunt it). The area taunts, Challenging Shout and Challenging Roar, have no single target and are not covered, a documented limit. The gate reads the subject's aura row and taunt immunity, so it refuses exactly the two subjects above (the Shade only at a bot without Delusions) and nothing else.

Limits of the gate: (a) none for a Delusions carrier any more: a tank that carries Delusions may taunt the Shade, since it can damage it, and keeps it as a target and as the warrior's Charge target; an uncursed tank is refused at the Shade and the "attacker of non-tank target" scan skips it, so the scan and the gate agree for both kinds of tank (the taunt-immunity check is unchanged and applies to a carrier too); (b) on a taunt-immune boss with a `taunt_after_cast` or tank-swap row (AQ20 Ossirian 15339 and Ayamiss 15369, `immunity_flags & 0x02`) the gated action also skips the off-tank's switch to the boss, because the old taunt never landed there anyway. Revisit when AQ runs resume.

The seek take's `LogSeekTake` row says `reason=taunt_gated` when the gate refuses the cast (see `docs/play/refusal-rows.md`).

### Taunts stand down at cast time

A generic single-target taunt is not cast while its mob already stands on another tank: the warrior's Taunt (the taunt row, the snare-target and off-target forms) and Mocking Blow, the druid's Growl, the paladin's Hand of Reckoning, the shaman's Earthshaker Slam (each with its on-attacker form). The "lose aggro" and "party member attacked" triggers already refuse a mob held by a tank when they queue the taunt, and each action re-checks the mob's current victim at cast time. "Lose aggro" stays quiet while a fresh mob has no victim yet (it picks a victim within a tick) and refuses a mob held by a tank once it has one, so the only taunts that reach the cast-time re-check are queued for a mob on a non-tank. It refuses any generic taunt on a mob another tank holds; this also closes the party snare-taunt path and the Arms Mocking Blow fallback, both wanted. A mob on the bot itself, on a pet or NPC, or on a non-tank player is unchanged. No flag. The AoE taunts re-check the "loose adds near" count the same way, uncached: Challenging Shout and Challenging Roar only fire while loose adds are still on a non-tank, re-walked at cast time so a shout queued for loose adds stands down once the pack settles inside the trigger value's one-second cache.

### Limits

The cast-time helper lives beside the taunt gate in the boss-tactics actions file; the boss-tactics taunts (tank swaps, seek takes, taunt-after) cast directly and never call it, so a planned swap still taunts off another tank. `IsTank` reads the forced role first and falls back to the talent read, so a DPS player mid stance dance or holding a shield can look like a tank. The paladin's Righteous Defense fallback has no such check and keeps its old rule.

### The threat successor

Behind `AiPlayerbot.DungeonRunThreatSuccessorNotHolder` (default `0`, recommended on): on a boss with a `threat_successor` row the damage dealers are capped at 80 % of a reference tank's threat. Without the key the reference is the highest assigned off-tank that is *hitting* the boss, even when the boss is hitting that off-tank (`ThreatCapTank.onBoss` reads `tank->GetVictim() == boss`, "this tank hits the boss", not "the boss hits this tank"). At Jin'do that capped the damage dealers against the holding off-tank while the main tank, whose threat an earlier Hex wiped, sat below them: after a Hex on the holding off-tank a damage dealer inherited the boss 39 times out of 40 (walk 55). With the key on, `ThreatCapTank.holdsBoss` (`boss->GetVictim() == tank`) is set and the reference is the **real** threat of the highest tank (main or off) that is hitting the boss and that the boss is **not** hitting. A tank that is not on the boss (a Hakkar off-tank on a Son, a Jin'do tank held on its Shade or in the pit) is no successor, while a tank wearing Jin'do's Hex (17172) counts as on the boss even though it cannot hit while polymorphed (the wiped tank stays the reference through the Hex), and when no tank qualifies the old pick stands exactly, away half-share included; so does a raid where only the holder is alive, on the map and uncharmed (a dead or charmed tank is left off the list; the safe check is map and instance only, there is no range check, `onBoss` is the guard). When nobody holds the boss (no victim, or a damage dealer holds it) every tank on the boss is a non-holder and the reference is the highest of them (the less safe choice for a second hand-back; revisit it if the ThreatCap rows show inheritors after a `holder=none` window). A 0 reference is not "no cap": on an engaged target `ThreatPctOf` reads it as 100 %, so the damage dealers are held. The `BossTacticsThreatCap` row carries `holder=<main|off|none>` (the tank the boss is hitting; none for a damage dealer or no victim).

Only bosses with a `threat_successor` row change, and only while a non-holding tank is on the boss.

Side effect, accepted: after a Hex on **either** tank the wiped tank is the reference at about 0 threat, so the damage dealers' single-target attacks are held until it has rebuilt past them. That lasts as long as the fight's threat needs (tens of seconds is possible, not a fixed 5-8 s), and a second Hex on the new holder before then still gives the boss to a damage dealer. The damage dealers keep working on adds (Shades, pit trolls, totems) meanwhile, Rogue Vanish and priest Fade on threat stay quiet on a 0 reference, and hunter pets cower (`ShouldPetCower`: pet threat above the tank's) against it too, so pets are held during the stall as well. A Hex handing Jin'do to a damage dealer is the top first-death cause, so the trade stands for walk 56, to be judged from the ThreatCap and Hex rows.

#### The reset window

Behind `AiPlayerbot.DungeonRunThreatResetWindow` (default `0`, A/B only, **not recommended**: tested in walk 68, made things worse; keep off): on a boss whose `threat_successor` row carries the reset spell (`cycle_spell`, Hakkar's Cause Insanity 24327), the damage dealers hold their damage on him around each expected reset. The timer is the row's: `param` and `window_s` are the earliest and latest gap between resets in seconds (19 and 26), and `priority` the hold percent (95); the reset time is the fight's charm scan. The window is open when the time since the last reset lies in `[k × 19 s, k × 26 s]` for k = 1, 2 or 3 (the 40-48 s double gaps included), so about 7 s of every 22.5 s. The reference is the highest threat among the tanks that do NOT hold Hakkar, on him or away on a Son (the cap's own pick requires `onBoss` and would drop that away tank); under 100 threat there is no hold.

A damage dealer (never a tank or a healer) at or above 95 % of that reference stops every damage action on Hakkar, specials and white damage, the swing and the auto-repeat included (the `threat reset white stop` trigger stops the running ones), and resumes under 90 %. Heals and cures are never held, an action aimed at another target keeps its damage, the target is never cleared, and DoT ticks already on him cannot be stopped. Expected: tank turns 61 → 84 % at best, realistically +7-12 points (0.45-0.75 more tank turns before 180 s, about 0.2-0.3 fewer non-tank deaths), against about 3-5 % of the damage on Hakkar; walk 57's ×0.90 counterfactual removed every walk-56 kill, so the net is uncertain and possibly negative. The A/B walk 68 made things worse: the tank share of Hakkar's victims after Cause Insanity fell from 58 % to 46 % overall (58 % → 49 % on casts 4+), the target was not met, and fewer bots were alive at 180 s (10.6 against 12.4) and 240 s (6.6 against 8.6), so keep it off. The `BossTacticsThreatReset` row (`reset hold: pct= ref= since_ms=`) names each bot's first hold per window, and the `BossTacticsCharm` row carries `win=` and `since_prev_ms=`.

**Rollback:** a binary from before this change refuses a `threat_successor` row with a nonzero `cycle_spell`, so on a migrated database it drops Hakkar's whole successor row, not only the timer. Run `tools/sql/rollback/20261109120000_world.rollback.sql` before starting one (outside `data/sql`, so the updater never applies it).

#### Cures skip the threat back-off

Behind `AiPlayerbot.DungeonRunCureIgnoresThreat` (default `0`, recommended on): a cure or dispel on a party member (dispel magic, cure poison/disease, remove curse, cleanse, purify) is exempt from the damage dealers' threat back-off, the same way heals are (`ThreatCapExempt`). Without it a healer whose own threat on Jin'do read at or over the 80 % line had its cure on the Hexed tank vetoed, and the Hex ran its full 5 s while a damage dealer held the boss (walk 56: 50 of 91 tank Hexes never cured; `dispel magic on party` result `I` in 10 of 12 failing frames). This is not limited to `threat_successor` bosses: every party cure on every fight. Range and line of sight still stop a cure. Measure in walk 57: tank Hexes cured within 1.5 s at least 70 % (walk 56: 32 %), Jin'do-on-DPS time after tank Hexes at most 120 s over 30 fights (252 s), and `dispel magic on party` `I` after a Hex at most 20 % (10 of 12).

#### A successor line that holds

Behind `AiPlayerbot.DungeonRunThreatSuccessorStrict` (default `0`, recommended on with the not-holder key), on a boss whose `threat_successor` row carries the mode word `strict` only. The shipped data sets it on Jin'do's row only (20261108220000): walk 57 ran the strict line on Hakkar's plain row too: Hakkar kills fell from 4/41 (walk 56) to 0/58, and Strict cost about 13 of the 18 points the damage dealers' damage on him fell. A successor row without `strict` keeps the plain cap with the key on or off: the projection escape, no white stop, the old multiplier exemption.

- **No projection escape.** The high-threat trigger no longer lets a damage dealer at or over 80 % of the reference keep attacking while its threat projects under the tank's in 5 s (`HighThreatProjectionEscapes`). That escape is why the damage dealers rode at 0.89-0.99 of a slowly climbing non-holding tank in walk 56, and why every Hex that went to a damage dealer found it at 1.00x the successor or more.
- **White damage stops near the line.** A running melee swing and a running auto-repeat (wand Shoot, Auto Shot) were never vetoed once started. At 92 % of the reference the `successor white cap` trigger stops the swing and interrupts the auto-repeat itself, in the trigger pass of the same decision cycle, so no higher action picked that cycle lets them run on (`SuccessorWhiteStopNow`). Only a swing or shot on the successor itself is stopped; one an action moved onto a Shade or an add is left alone (`SuccessorWhiteOnTarget`). Each mode is judged on its own target (`SuccessorWhiteStopsOf`): a swing on the boss stops while a wand on an add keeps shooting, and the reverse. The trigger is checked on minimal (throttled) ticks too: its node carries a never-queued no-op handler at relevance 100. While the per-bot latch holds (`SuccessorWhiteLatch`) it stops them again on every pass. Melee and wand start again under 85 %; a hunter's Auto Shot under 80 %, because its restart is a single-target action that the 80 % veto still gates. Specials keep the 80 % line. Tanks are never stopped, and a target without a `strict` successor row clears the latch.
- **Not during a Hex window.** While the reference is 0 (a tank Hex just wiped the successor) white damage is never stopped and the latch clears; the specials stay held by the 0-reference veto as before. The `BossTacticsThreatCap` row carries `white=held|free ref0=0|1` under the key, so walk 57 can split the Hex-window cost from the 92 % stop.
- **Not covered:** DoT ticks already on the boss cannot be stopped; only the re-cast is vetoed at 80 %. Another attack action (a reach, a target switch, a strategy's own attack) can start the swing again during a hold; the trigger stops it again on its next pass, so a few swings can land in between.
- **Rollback:** a binary from before the `strict` word refuses Jin'do's row and loses his whole successor cap; run `tools/sql/rollback/20261108220000_world.rollback.sql` before starting one on a migrated database.

The 65 % white stop removed in 405801e86 cost a fifth of the raid's damage; this one keeps the damage dealers between 80 % and 92 % of the successor. The guard for walk 57: raid damage per second on Jin'do at 92 % of walk 56 or more. The aims: top damage dealer / successor at a tank Hex, median, at most 0.90 (walk 56: about 1.05); Hexes on a tank holder that go to a damage dealer at most 35 % (66 of 91; off-tank holder 76 %); pure-overtake time with Jin'do on a damage dealer at most 80 s (227 s).

#### Cursed tanks stay in the pick

Behind `AiPlayerbot.DungeonRunThreatSuccessorKeepsAway` (default `0`, recommended on with the not-holder key): a tank wearing Delusions of Jin'do (24306) stands on its Shade by design and keeps its Jin'do threat, so it counts as on the boss, as a Hexed tank already does (`ThreatCapCursedOnBoss`). It counts only while its threat is above 0 and at least half the highest tank's: Delusions is never cured, and a cursed tank with little or no Jin'do threat would otherwise be a near-0 reference that holds every damage dealer for the whole curse. Walk 56: 12 of 39 off-tank Hexes that went to a damage dealer had `ref=offtank holder=off` before the Hex, against 1 of 10 that went to a tank; a cursed main tank on a Shade was one cause.

A charmed tank (Brain Wash, Cause Insanity) is still left out of the pick: it is off the boss's threat list, and its old threat would lift the cap above anything the boss can turn to (on Hakkar that is the walk-41 failure). A main tank that stays idle after a charm (`select new target` / `reach spell` loops) is tank targeting, not the threat pick, and is not covered. Known limit: a cursed tank at half the holder's threat or more stays the reference while its Jin'do threat stands still on its Shade, so the damage dealers stay held at its line (80 %, white damage at 92 % under the strict key) until the holder doubles it, which can take tens of seconds. That is the not-holder design (the cursed tank is the real next victim after a Hex on the holder). Walk 57 splits these rows out of the damage guard: `BossTacticsThreatCap` rows with `ref=offtank holder=main offtank_target=Shade of Jin'do white=held`. Measure in walk 57: pre-Hex `ref=offtank holder=off` rows in off-holder windows (walk 56: 20 %), off-holder Hexes with `mt_threat` 0 (10 of 47).

### Reaction and Hex rows

Two log rows make the Jin'do fight measurable on the server clock (no flag; they cost nothing without `bot_events.csv`):

- `BossTacticsKillFirst reason=switch` (info2 `pick=<entry>:<guid low>,from=<entry|0>,since_ms=<ms>`): a Delusions carrier turned onto a needs_aura Shade (14986). `since_ms` is the time since this bot's pick first saw that Shade (`CarrierSwitchSinceMs`; first sight is kept per bot and Shade in a small bounded map). A record is dropped only 3 s after the bot's pick last listed the Shade (it died, left, the curse or the fight ended), not on a single missed pass; past 512 records the oldest quarter goes. One row per bot per Shade, written by the kill-first action only (a Shade reached by the carrier strike writes none). A Shade only the carrier scan (`AiPlayerbot.DungeonRunCarrierScan`) found, and that the attackers list and the kill-first grid both missed, has no first-sight record and writes no row.
- `BossTacticsHex` (info1 `holder=<main|off|dps> ot_threat=<n> mt_threat=<n> top_dps=<name>:<threat>`): Jin'do's Hex (17172) landed on a member. `holder` is the member who **wore the Hex** (not the boss's victim; the field keeps its name because the walk-report parser reads `holder=`, and it is a different thing from `holder=` in the ThreatCap row). The wearer is found by its aura among the raid's tanks and then the group's members, once per second per boss, because the boss has already turned to someone else when the row is written. The threats are read from the boss's threat manager right after (the Hex wipes the wearer's threat): `ot_threat` is the wearer's when an off-tank wore it, else the first off-tank's; `mt_threat` the main tank's; `-1` when the raid's tank order has no such tank (a dead tank, or one off the threat list, reads what the boss holds for it, usually 0); `top_dps` the highest non-tank, non-healer. One row per member and Hex. It is written on the world thread at the next read of the boss's tank threat by any bot (a damage dealer, priest or rogue trigger), not at the aura apply: the aura-watch hook of the combat timeline runs on a map thread behind `AiPlayerbot.CombatTimeline` and has no bot to log under, so the row is logged under whichever bot read the threat.

### Shade cover row

`BossTacticsShadeCover` (no flag; it costs nothing without `bot_events.csv`) measures the top wipe opener of walk 56: a Shade copies Jin'do's threat list, where heal threat is top, so it walks to a healer, and 50 of 59 healers a Shade killed were not cursed and could not hurt it. A Delusions carrier runs the check with its kill-first trigger, over the whole group: for every living Shade it sees (its attackers list and the kill-first grid read) whose victim is a healer of the group, every cursed damage dealer and every cursed off-tank that does not hold the boss and is not on that Shade gets a row, logged under that member. Healers keep their own carrier-strike guards and the main tank never leaves the boss, so neither is named. info1 is `reason=<word>,role=<dps|offtank>`, with the first word that fits: `dead`, `charmed` (the Brain Wash Totem), `other_shade` (already on another Shade), `out_of_range` (more than 30 yd from this one, or more than 6 yd above or below it: the troll pit), `threat_gate` (an off-tank on a `threat_successor` fight, which the carrier strike keeps on the boss; never while `AiPlayerbot.DungeonRunShadeHealerCover` and `AiPlayerbot.DungeonRunCarrierStrike` are both on), `guard` (with both keys on, an off-tank the carrier strike's guards hold back: the main tank under 60 % or its own health under `AiPlayerbot.LowHealth`; the row does not read the other members' health the strike also counts), `on_boss`, `other_target` (a ward, a totem, a troll) or `no_target`. info2 is `shade=<entry>:<guid low>,healer=<name>,dist=<yd>,target=<entry>:<guid low>|none`. A miss is logged once it has lasted 2 s, then at most once per member and Shade per 5 s; a member on the Shade drops its record. The record map is bounded at 512 pairs: when it is full, records not noted for 3 s go first, then the oldest quarter (`ShadeCoverLogBook`). The curse drops at death, so `dead` only names a member that was already missing the Shade when it died, and only once per Shade. A Shade on a cursed healer counts as a cover target too: that healer's own strike guards decide whether it hits back.

Limits of the row: (a) with `bot_events.csv` on, every carrier repeats the whole-group pass once a second and reads the kill-first grid for the Shade's entry even when `AiPlayerbot.DungeonRunKillFirstGrid` is off; acceptable on a test server, not meant for a live realm with the csv on; (b) "holds the boss" is read from the logging carrier's own fight list, so a carrier that does not list Jin'do can name the off-tank holding him.

### Shade healer cover

Behind `AiPlayerbot.DungeonRunShadeHealerCover` (default `0`, recommended on): the carriers cover the healers a Shade attacks. The carrier pick (`PickCarrierOverride`: a DPS under the run's own order, the carrier strike, the run's focus skip) ranks a Shade whose victim is a healer of the group, that stands within 30 yd of the carrier and on the carrier's floor (no more than 6 yd above or below it, `kCarrierPitDropYd`, so nobody is sent into the troll pit), ahead of every other Shade, the nearer of them first; at equal distance the first one found stays, and past 30 yd or on another floor the nearest Shade wins as before. A free DPS already ranks a Shade on a healer first (`PickKillFirst`). With `AiPlayerbot.DungeonRunCarrierStrike` on, a cursed off-tank the boss is not hitting is let through the `threat_successor` rule when its pick is such a Shade (`CarrierStrikeInput.shadeCover`) and its own threat on the `threat_successor` boss is above 0 and at least half the highest tank's (`ShadeCoverThreatAllows`, the `kThreatCapOffTankAwayShare` line). Below that line (a Hex wiped it) it stays on the boss as the successor reference, cover or not. Once it hits the Shade, the Shade usually turns to it: the cover stays latched for that off-tank and that Shade while the Shade's victim is the off-tank itself, until the Shade dies, the off-tank loses the curse, or 15 s pass since the Shade was last on a healer (`ShadeCoverLatchHolds`). While the latch holds, the latched Shade is the strike's pick before any other (`FindBossCarrierStrikePick` resolves it first), so a nearer Shade does not take the off-tank away from the one it pulled. Under the cover the lowest-member line leaves out only the Shade's victim (the healer being eaten): the off-tank's own health and every other member's still hold it home, and so does the main-tank line (60 %). An off-tank the boss is hitting stays (`threat_successor`, then `holds_boss`), the main tank never leaves, a cursed healer keeps all of its own guards, and an uncursed bot never picks a Shade (the fight scan drops the gated row for it). With the key off the pick and the strike are exactly as before.

The off-tank half pairs with `AiPlayerbot.DungeonRunThreatSuccessorKeepsAway`, and uses the same line. That key keeps a cursed tank that is off the boss on the successor list while its threat is above 0 and at least 50 % of the top tank's; the cover only lets an off-tank leave while it is above that same line. With KeepsAway on, an off-tank on its Shade therefore stays the threat successor at the threat it left with. With KeepsAway off (or where that key does not exist), an off-tank on a Shade is no successor while it is there, and the damage dealers cap against the remaining tanks; the half-share line still keeps a Hex-wiped off-tank on the boss. Turn the cover on together with `DungeonRunThreatSuccessorKeepsAway`.

Limits of the cover: (a) the 15 s latch runs from the last time the Shade was on a healer and is not extended while the Shade stays on the off-tank, so a clean pull can drop the cover mid-kill; the strike then stops, the Shade keeps hitting the off-tank (the healer is safe), and the cover takes it again once the Shade turns back to a healer; (b) no hysteresis: a Shade whose victim flips between a healer and a damage dealer flips a cursed DPS's pick between it and a nearer Shade, each flip costing a retarget, and the grid's victim read is up to 1 s old, so an off-tank can idle a tick on a Shade that just left its healer; (c) the carrier scan (`AiPlayerbot.DungeonRunCarrierScan`) takes the nearest Shade it finds and ignores the healer ranking; it only runs when the ordinary pick saw no Shade at all.

### Brain Wash Totem with a charmed main tank

Behind `AiPlayerbot.DungeonRunFactJudgeCharmedLead` (default `0`, recommended on), with `AiPlayerbot.DungeonRunTotemFocus`: the Brain Wash Totem stays a kill-first fact while the run's lead bot (the main tank) is the one Brain-Washed. The run reads its fight against the lead bot, and a charmed lead is friendly to its charmer's side, so Jin'do as the totem's owner read as not engaged, the engaged units came back empty (the fight read as stale), the raid guard lost its attackers, and the run's own focus order was refused as "friendly": the totem focus went silent for the whole charm. Walk 57: 16 of 19 main-tank charm windows of 10 s or more had no `BossTacticsTotemFocus` row, the main tank stayed charmed for a median 86 s and meleed the raid, and 16 of 35 off-holder Hexes that went to a damage dealer had the main tank charmed. With the key on, while the lead is charmed and a living, uncharmed commanded bot stands on its map (`RunJudge`, the judge `GroupCanAttack` already used; real players are never the judge), that bot judges instead (`RunFactJudgeReplacesLead`): the kill-first friendliness test and the owner's engagement, the engaged units, the crowd-control hold read, the raid guard's anchor and the order's target check. The totem focus itself still sends the damage dealers with the lead as the selection owner: each bot judges the totem by itself. With the whole party charmed nothing changes, and with the key off the charmed lead judges as before. It is generic: any charm on the lead, in every raid and dungeon, with no boss id. Walk-58 measures: main-tank charm windows of 10 s or more with no TotemFocus row 84 % -> 20 % or less; main-tank charm median 86 s -> 25 s or less; off-holder Hex -> damage dealer with the main tank charmed 16/35 -> 4 or fewer.

## Known limits

Recurring threat-reset holds need a cast trigger. A `dps_hold_after` row with `spell_id` 0 fires only its engage edge, so it serves the opening 5 s Hakkar pull hold only and never comes back mid-fight (the spell-less Mandokir row was removed). Hakkar's Cause Insanity is answered by the `threat_successor` row's cap at the assigned off-tanks, which is what keeps the boss off the damage dealers; like Jin'do he is immune to taunts, so his `taunt_after_cast` row was deleted by 20261107200000 and his section above lists no taunt row. By contrast, a spell-less `taunt_after_cast` mode `until_tank` covers a scripted threat reset: Gurubashi Knock Away and Intimidating Roar leave the Berserker on a non-tank victim, which the episode watches for directly, so no cast slot is needed (see the Zul'Gurub table above). An instant teleport leaves no cast to read either, so a keyed `until_tank` row (`taunt_after_cast` with a `spell_id`) opens on its tank victim's displacement instead. Jin'do's Banish 24466 row did that until 20261106120000 deleted it: like Hakkar he is immune to taunts, so no taunt row can take him back; his `threat_successor` row and the `tank_ignore` troll row do that work instead.

### Taunt-immune boss victim escape

Not a row kind of its own: `AiPlayerbot.DungeonRunImmuneBossVictimEscape` (default `0`, recommended on) serves every raid and dungeon and names no boss. Its scope is a boss the game makes immune to taunts that also carries a `threat_successor` boss-tactic row (Hakkar and Jin'do today; another boss opts in by such a row, and fixate bosses such as Buru stay out). A victim that is not a tank escapes at once with its own ability when the bot's full cast check passes (hunter Feign Death, rogue Vanish, mage Ice Block for Frost mages, paladin Divine Shield); with none castable (or a real player), a paladin within 30 yd casts Blessing of Protection (Hand of Protection on this core) on it. The game skips a victim immune to the boss's melee when it picks its next target, so the boss turns away; Feign Death and Vanish drop the victim's threat. Feign Death can be resisted: an escape that did not land (no feign-death state, no immunity) holds the victim 1 s, then the paladin may protect. Fade is not used: on this core it is a dummy aura with no threat effect. Never on a tank, at most one landed escape per victim per 6 s, never when the victim is the boss's only living non-tank target, and never while it holds a duty: the walk-out puller, a member holding a pulled Son, or the victim of another live, engaged hostile creature that is not a boss (a Son, an add). The reaction-engine copy rides on `avoid aoe`, so a fight strategy that drops it (Vaelastrasz) keeps only the combat-engine copy. Row `BossTacticsVictimEscape` (`boss=,victim=,class=,spell=,result=ok|refused`, `reason=own|protect|cast|duty|only_target`). A protected member cannot attack for 10 s, and the boss may chain to the next damage dealer.

| `map` / `boss_entry` | Where and whose fight. |
| `anchor` | Fight spot plus `x y r`: the boss at it within `r`. |
| `role` | `ranged`, `healer`, or `melee`. Tanks count as melee; healers seat first. |
| `window_spell` | `0` means always. |
| `aura` | Aura gating the stand. |
| `side` + `seq` / `x y z` | Seat position. `z` is put back on the ground. |
| `dx dy` | Slots 1-8 (default 5): a line of seats 9 yd apart. |
| `window_signal` | `cast` or `stopped_no_target`: still, targetless, with aura. `stopped_no_target` needs `aura` plus `window_spell`. |
| `hold_ms` | Default 8000, range 1000-15000, or until `release_anchor`. |
| `release_anchor` | Anchor that ends the stand. |

Primary key is `(map, boss_entry, anchor, role, window_spell, side, seq)`. Unusable rows are skipped with a log; a bad `release_anchor` for a boss drops that boss's set.

Seats go to same-role bots in id order. Taking part means being on the threat list or victim within 80 yd of the anchor, or holding a stand. Sides come from the lane cross product. While seated, the stand action walks the bot to its seat. After 3 refusals the seat is given up and reach or dodge takes over; seated bots suppress reach-spell, heal walk, dragon flank, and raid spread, and, with `AiPlayerbot.BossStandOutranksBreathDodge = 1` (default `0`, recommended on), the Onyxia breath dodge: a stand seat already stands clear of its breath lane, so a seated bot keeps it.

With `AiPlayerbot.BossStandWholeRaid = 1` (default `0`, recommended on), a bot in combat with the boss counts toward the seats even after a threat wipe (threat 0, not the victim), a bot that already stands on its seat's side of the lane and at least as far from the lane line as the seats next to it along the lane (within 3 yd of its own along-lane spot, every role; with none it walks) stays where it stands, and a seated bot does not flee: the stand row's `stay=1` names a stay, and a stay never yields a tank post.

With `AiPlayerbot.BossStandEvents = 1` (default `0`), every bot logs one `BossStand` episode per window: `assigned` (yards to its seat, whether it stayed where it stood, and whether the window is open or held), `arrived` (ms since the assignment), `unseated` once with the reason (`not_counted`, `given_up`, `side_full`) and `released` with the reason (`other_window`, `no_boss`, `release_anchor`, `hold_time`, `dead`, `gone`). A walk can then tell a bot that never got a seat from one that got it late or walked off it. Needs `bot_events.csv`; with the key off nothing is written.

## Adding a boss

- Read the boss from the core: creature template, spells, events, scripts, spell template. Use Turtle ids only.
- Add rows in a NEW migration `data/sql/world/YYYYMMDDHHMMSS_world.sql` (idempotent; never edit an applied one).
- Run the SQL tests with the core checkout set: `tools/test_boss_tactics_sql.py`, `tools/test_boss_stand_sql.py`.
- Check in game with `DungeonTactics = 1` or a commander binding, then a boss pull.
